# Load transition

> **What:** first-load gates, reload swaps, graph card list swaps, row entrances and accordion reveals for pages, boards and data-backed modal content. **Read when:** a load, refresh, selector, page or modal switch changes visible data, or a section expands or collapses in place.

Status: **current**, 2026-10-07. Provenance: #30, #60, #61, #70, #71, #149, #169, #177, #260, #261, #270, #304, #316, #323, #325, #561, #593, #614, #616.

Owner-approved variant (#561): in-place expand/collapse (accordions, switch reveals, selector panels) is not a load; it opens, then fades its content in, and fades out, then closes, under [accordion](accordion.md). These rules still govern every load and reload.

## Intent

Loading must communicate a deliberate state change rather than a hard cut: first loads gate incomplete content, reloads replace stale content through a spinner, and row entrances run once per reveal rather than while the reader scrolls.

## Rules

- **R1. Separate first load from reload.** A first-load gate shows one spinner until the initial content is ready; a selector, page or retry reload uses the shared swap rather than a local Boolean or skeleton implementation. Nothing comes before that spinner: a page that must read settings before it can build its content (Android Leaderboards overview, #178) counts unread settings as not ready in its swap, so the first load shows the spinner, keeps it while its first data loads, and reveals with the swap's entrance. It never shows an empty body. Page tools that act on the gated content (Song Details Paths, Quick Links, the Item Shop action) wait for the same gate: none shows, or reaches the screen reader, while the spinner is up. They appear with the revealed content, together so the toolbar does not grow in steps, and stay while shown content refreshes (#585; web gates Quick Links on `LoadPhase.ContentIn`, and the owner asked the same for Paths). Android Song Details reads one signal for its toolbar tools: the plan the revealed page publishes from inside `FestivalLoadGate`. Tests: `SongsUiTest.songDetailPageToolsWaitForTheLoadGate` and the `@DeviceCi` `SongDetailPageToolsAccessibilityJourneyTest`. Android pages whose Quick Links act on a load swap's content pass sections only while that swap shows content (#649): Compete and the Leaderboards overview while `swap.showsContent` (so a Rank By reload hides Quick Links with the cards, as web `ContentIn` does), and the Rivals hub while its tab's swap is at `ContentIn` (not during the spinner's fade-out, nor the old tab's fade-out on a tab switch). Android Paths' "Some Instruments Unavailable" alert likewise waits for the sheet's first settled load and never covers its spinner (owner, #649; the web opens it with the modal). Tests: `LeaderboardsUiTest.overviewQuickLinksWaitForTheLoadGate`, `RivalsUiTest.hubQuickLinksNeverShowBesideTheSpinner`, `SongPathsSheetUiTest.karaokeWarningWaitsForTheFirstPathToLoad`, device `LoadSwapAccessibilityJourneyTest.leaderboardsQuickLinksArriveWithTheCards`, `RivalsHubQuickLinksAccessibilityJourneyTest` and `SongPathsDeviceTest.karaokeWarningWaitsForTheFirstPath`. Apple Song Details (every form factor) resolves Item Shop, Paths and Quick Links from its `ready` gate in one policy, `SongDetailPageTools.resolve(contentShown:…)`, for the iPhone accessory and the iPad/Mac/Duo toolbar alike. Every read that decides whether a tool applies belongs to that gate: Apple's first load awaits the Item Shop feed with the cards, and a failed Shop read completes it as "no offer" (the page shows its Shop status notice), so the Item Shop action never joins after Paths and Quick Links; later Shop changes (a new publication, Hide Item Shop turned off) are read only once the page shows. The Mac/iPad **Song** menu keeps *Paths…* and *Open in Item Shop* listed but disabled until then (HIG *The menu bar*: disable rather than hide). Hosted test: `SongDetailPageToolsGateTests` (holds the Shop response past the card reads, and fails it). A notice about gated content waits for it the same way. The Paths sheet's Karaoke "Some Instruments Unavailable" alert appears once per opening only after every form on screen has replaced its spinner with its image, table or error, never over the spinner (owner, #649: "Paths shows 'Karaoke not supported' modal before content loads"). This departs from web `PathsModal`, which raises the alert on open. HIG Alerts: "Prefer conveying information in context" (best practice). Apple: `SongPathsSheet.presentsUnavailableWarning` (every form factor). Tests: hosted `SongPathsSwitchAccessibilityTests.karaokeNoticeWaitsForTheChartOnScreen`; the journey `PathsKaraokeNoticeAccessibilityJourneyTests` holds the opening's path reads with the fixture's `hold-paths` control and requires only the named spinner and no notice until they resolve, then the notice with the chart behind it and no spinner (iPhone and iPad at Large and AX5, simulated folded iPhone Duo, and Duo book pose with the image loaded beside the held table); the iPhone journey `SongDetailJourneyTests.testPathUnavailableWarningCanPersistAcrossColdLaunch`. Every Paths journey checks the same state the moment the notice shows (`PathsUITestSupport.waitForKaraokeNotice`). Windows gates Quick Links in the canonical model: `QuickLinksViewModel(contentShown: false)` keeps `IsAvailable` false until the page sets `IsContentShown` with its first reveal, and never clears it on a reload (Leaderboards opens it on its swap's `ContentRevealed`, after the spinner's fade-out, not at `IsReady`, which would lead the cards by that fade; `QuickLinksHost` fades the entry points in after the layout that realizes the cards, #649; Song Details, Band Details, Profile and Rival Detail already host Quick Links inside their gated content). The Paths dialog's Karaoke notice is likewise content-gated: an inline `InfoBar` that `SongPathsViewModel.RevealWarning` opens only after the first path commits (#649; was a `ContentDialog` before the chart). A first-load gate waits only for the reads that define its first screen, never a selected player's own rows or one slow board: Windows Leaderboards reveals when its first four instrument cards' top tens settle, or `LeaderboardsViewModel.RevealDeadline` (1 s) after the first of them settles, and a later card keeps its own skeleton (#649; web's overview load phase waits on the top tens only). Tests: `LeaderboardsOverviewTests.QuickLinksAppearWithTheFirstRevealAndStayThroughReloads`, `…QuickLinksWaitForTheCardsEntranceAfterTheSpinnerFades`, `…RevealDoesNotWaitForTheSelectedPlayersOwnRows`, `…ASlowFirstScreenfulBoardRevealsAfterTheDeadline`, `QuickLinksTests.ViewModel_GatedOnContentHidesTheEntryUntilTheContentShows`, `SongPathsViewModelTests.Warning_WaitsForTheFirstReadAndSurvivesAnEarlyClose`; UIA `a11y-loading-placement.json` `leaderboards-loading-centred` / `leaderboards-reload-centred` and `a11y-modals.json` `modal-paths-warning`.
- **R2. Reloads are latest-wins.** Hide or fade the old result, show the spinner while the newest request settles, fade the spinner, then reveal the committed result and its row stagger. Apple’s immediate old-content removal is the approved #71 variant that prevents new labels on stale rows. Stale content never shows under the spinner: content that keeps its slot beside the spinner (a pinned "your score" row whose slot holds the pager in place, #93) is invisible, absent from the accessibility tree and ignores touches until the new result commits, with or without reduced motion (Android `LoadSwap.pinnedContentModifier` with `pinnedStaggered`, opt-in for content kept composed under the spinner only: the song and song band leaderboards' pinned rows, #149, keyed by `rememberLoadSwap(pinnedKey = …)` since #190, and the Leaderboards overview list that keeps loading its cards there, #178, which hides on every reload; Windows `LoadSwapGate` + `PinnedRowGate` + `PinnedRowReveal` on the song leaderboard, #295, the song band leaderboard, #306 and #307, and Full Rankings, #270). A pinned row is stale only when the pending request can change it: like the web footer (`PaginatedLeaderboard` re-animates only when `footerAnimKey` changes; Full Rankings passes its instrument + Rank By `cacheKey`, never the page), a page-only, retry or refresh reload keeps it visible and usable beside the spinner, without a fade or re-entrance; it still fades in with the first rows on the first load (#295) (Windows agent decision below; Android owner decision, #190). The spinner is one element: the system busy indicator, named for what is loading ("Loading Item Shop"); never wrap it in a role-less element. Where a live region names the load, that region is the spinner's one stop: Android `SongPathsSheet` puts the indeterminate progress on its `fst.paths.status` node and clears the indicator's own semantics, which Compose would otherwise keep as a second, unnamed "In progress" stop (#506, device `PathsSwapAccessibilityJourneyTest`). Assistive technology reads whatever the gate keeps (a retained header) before the spinner, and the gate never reorders the controls around it (Apple `FestivalLoadingView` labels the `ProgressView` itself; `FestivalReloadGate` sorts its retained frame first inside its own container, #431). HIG VoiceOver: "be sure to keep labels current as interface and content change".
- **R3. Use the shared timings.** Board/page swaps use 300 ms content-out and 500 ms spinner-out; Apple gates retain the web 150 ms spinner-in and 400 ms minimum spinner. Paths uses 300 ms fades (web `opacity 300ms ease`) with a 400 ms image or 500 ms text minimum. The Paths table then reveals by its row stagger rather than a container fade (web `PathDataTable` `textStagger`: each row's 400 ms `fadeInUp` starts 60 ms after the previous one, `STAGGER_ROW_MS`, uncapped). It still fades out as a whole (Android #177 and Windows `FadeIn.Stagger`). Agent decision (#177, 2026-10-06): the Paths content, message and spinner fades use the web CSS `ease` curve (`cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.1, 0.25, 1)`, Compose `Ease`), not the M3 Standard curve (`cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`) nor the earlier Android linear fade. Material 3's transition table ("Standard | 300ms | Begin and end on screen (utility)") is a recommendation, not a requirement, and the duration is already met; web `PathsModal` sets `opacity 300ms ease`, and web semantics beat undocumented native copies (Android's linear fade, Apple's `.easeInOut`), so this keeps what the web reader sees: about 80% faded in halfway through, where Standard is about 88% and linear 50%. The `SongPathsSheetUiTest` image fade test fails if the curve goes back to linear. Owner may override. The publication refresh (R9) runs all four phases: content out 300 ms → spinner in 150 ms, held ≥400 ms and until the page has prepared for the new generation → spinner out 500 ms → rebuilt content in. The 400 ms minimum counts from the spinner's appearance and includes its 150 ms fade-in (web `MIN_SPINNER_MS` measured from the spinner's start in `PathsModal`; Apple `PublicationRefreshTransition` starts the wait with the fade), so with data already ready the spinner sits at full opacity for at least 250 ms. Data that is ready during content-out never skips the spinner. Under reduced motion the fades are 0 ms but the 400 ms hold stays (R6). Android: `rememberLoadSwap(holdSpinner = true)` (#616 review; `LoadSwapPolicy.MIN_SPINNER_MS`, `SPINNER_IN_MS`).
- **R4. Keep controls usable.** Pickers, pagers and modal selectors live outside the gated result area; a new selection supersedes pending work instead of waiting for it. A pager stays visible and tappable while the next page loads, showing the last loaded page count until the new one commits (web `placeholderData: (previous) => previous`); it hides only for the first load and on failure. It never shows before a loaded board has given its page count (no placeholder "1 / 1"), and never for a single page (web `hasPagination = !!data && totalPages > 1`, #575). A switch that opens a new board route (the song leaderboard's instrument switch) is that board's first load, so its pager waits for the new count (web `keepPreviousLeaderboardPage` keeps only the same song and instrument). A switch that changes the board in place (instrument, Rank By, band size, a player's band group) also leaves the new count unknown, so on Windows the pager hides from the switch until the new board's count commits instead of showing the previous board's count (agent decision #575, below; web `FullRankingsPage` keeps `placeholderData` only while instrument and metric are unchanged; the owner may override). Page-only reloads always keep the pager and its count. The stale-content hiding of R2 never applies to controls (Android band pagers, #149). A page header naming what is loaded (the song leaderboard header, [song-leaderboard-header](song-leaderboard-header.md) R5) also stays outside the gate: it appears with the first load and stays still while pages change, and only the rows swap (web `LeaderboardPage` keeps `SongInfoHeader` outside its LoadGate and skips its stagger on `'paginate'`, #316). Apple keeps a header that shares the rows' scroll view through `FestivalReloadGate`'s retained-frame form; Android and Windows hold it outside their swap. A failed reload keeps that header too: the error and Retry replace only the rows beneath it (web renders `EmptyState` in the result area under `SongInfoHeader`); only a failed first load, with no header yet, fills the page. A reload must not shrink the scroll content enough to clamp a header that has scrolled under the bar back into view ([song-leaderboard-header](song-leaderboard-header.md) R3).
- **R5. A scroll rushes the first-load stagger and never replays it.** Every page has one fade window for its first-load entrance. Like the web `useStaggerRush` (installed for every page by `Page.tsx`, and by `SearchModal`), any scroll while the entrance is running starts every fade that hasn't begun yet. That includes a drag, a fling, a Quick Links or section jump, and the programmatic jump to a selected row. The remaining content then fades in together (one 400 ms `fadeInUp`) instead of finishing its stagger. Content that the scroll realizes while the entrance runs joins it rather than appearing opaque (#323). Fades that have already started keep running. Once the entrance has finished, lazily realized content appears immediately, so scrolling never replays an entrance. A list window that limits fades to its load (sections that start loading when scrolled to) also shows content loaded after the scroll in place. Suggestions alone fades newly generated batches: only its first batch is rushed, because web `SuggestionsPage` doesn't install the rush. A selected-row reveal (web `navToPlayer`/`navToBand`: the song leaderboard, the song band leaderboard and Full Rankings) scrolls only after that row's own entrance, and doesn't scroll if the reader has already scrolled. Apple re-arms the Suggestions window for each new batch, so a scroll rushes only that batch's entrances. The rows a selected-row reveal reaches fade in with its rush even when it starts as the stagger ends, and a row a lazy list builds below the viewport ahead of time never spends its entrance unseen (#351 review).
- **R6. Honor reduced motion.** Motion-disabled platforms swap without fades or stagger; retain only the minimum spinner hold needed to avoid a blink. An automatic scroll to a selected row is instant and does not wait for an entrance. HIG Motion: "Add motion purposefully; gratuitous or excessive animation distracts and can cause physical discomfort." MD3: the easing/duration system is used for "transitions (entering, exiting, shared-axis)."
- **R7. Graph card lists follow the web list sequence.** When a graph card's selector changes already-loaded data, its list (Score History's best scores, inside the same card after the graph on every native platform: Apple #381, Android and Windows #588) is not a reload and shows no spinner. The old rows fade out and drift up 8 units (150 ms ease-in, 40 ms stagger; the phase lasts 200 + 40 × (rows − 1) ms). Then the list eases to its new height over 300 ms (`ease`) with no rows visible, and the new rows fade in from 12 units below (300 ms ease-out, 60 ms stagger). A newer change cancels the running one, and the same rows update in place. The list follows the selection at once rather than a graph fade, and the card's View All button sits after the list, toggling with the selection as in the web `GraphCard`. Reduced motion swaps at once (R6). Supersedes (#621): an older copy of this rule put the list under the card with View All outside it, which every native platform replaced with the one-card layout (#381, #588).
- **R8. Graph card chart swaps keep the card's size.** When a graph card's selector switches charts, the graph fades out (150 ms), swaps and fades in (250 ms) while the graph keeps its width and height, so nothing below it moves (owner, #61). Where the list shares the graph's card (Apple #381, Android and Windows #588), the graph section is what keeps its size; only the list after it resizes, by R7. Rows only some charts need stay reserved on every chart (the pager row while any selectable chart pages). The new chart opens with no bar selected (web `useChartPagination`), and a detail row the switch closes keeps its space, empty, until the reader picks or clears a bar or the data reloads. Agent decision (#261, 2026-10-05): reserve rather than ease to the shorter height (the web detail-card shrink moved the content below) or carry the selection over (no web precedent; a screen reader would announce a bar the reader never picked); owner may override. Reduced motion swaps at once and keeps the same reserved rows (R6). The page doesn't scroll during the swap, even when the list after the graph changes length: the graph stays where the reader picked the chart, and only the list and the content below it move (#423: Windows scroll anchoring scrolled the card off the top). Picking or clearing a bar, which opens or closes its detail row below the chart, doesn't scroll the page either (#512: Windows anchoring pushed the chart under the pinned header, where Left/Right no longer reached the next bar).
- **R9. A publication refresh keeps the reader on the page.** A new publication refreshes every open page and route in place through the shared publication boundary: no pop to a root, no explanatory card. While old content is hidden behind the spinner, VoiceOver keeps a stable page anchor (an invisible heading named with the page title, value "Loading new scores"); focus moves there only if it was established to be inside the page (on screen, uncovered, in the page's hierarchy and frame), and returns there after the rebuild unless the reader moved elsewhere during the spinner. Focus elsewhere (tab bar, navigation bar, a sheet, another column) is never moved; one on-screen page announces "Loading new scores" per publication instead. Where the platform cannot report the VoiceOver cursor (macOS), always announce and never move focus. Pages inside the boundary therefore set their title with `festivalNavigationTitle`. A root whose search field and page tools must stay usable (Apple Songs, #304 review) wraps only its result area, names the anchor with the boundary's `title`, reads the new generation in `prepare` (never from a revision-keyed task, so the fading list never draws new rows) and reveals through `onReveal`; its own reload gate is rebuilt already loaded, so the refresh shows one spinner, not two. When those results are a UIKit-backed `List`, it also passes `retainsHiddenContent: false`: `accessibilityHidden` does not reach `List` rows, so the faded-out list unmounts under the spinner rather than leaving old rows readable. The boundary never re-runs its page for its own focus bookkeeping: Apple keeps the `@AccessibilityFocusState`, spinner and page anchor in the `PublicationRefreshFocusLayer` overlay, because the accessibility focus store invalidates the view that owns that state while a list scrolls, and on the boundary each pass rebuilt the whole page mid-gesture (Songs title and rows jumped, #325). HIG Focus and selection: "Avoid changing focus without people's interaction", with the exception "if the focused item disappears during discrete, directional input (keyboard, remote, game controller), moving focus to an item one step away keeps it findable" (VoiceOver swipes are discrete, directional input; the anchor is the nearest surviving item); HIG VoiceOver: "Announce visible content and layout changes."
- **R10. Accordions grow, then fade in; fade out, then collapse.** Owner-approved variant (#561, 2026-10-09), scoped to accordion-style reveals: a section, panel or detail row that opens in place under its header, chip or picker (filter-sheet groups, Suggestions Instrument-Specific switches, the Instrument Selector panel, Paths panels, the Score History detail row, Settings options that a switch reveals). Loads, reloads, menus, popups and page transitions keep R1–R9. Opening grows the container to its content's height over 150 ms with the content hidden, then fades the content in over 150 ms; closing fades the content out over 150 ms at full height, then collapses the container over 150 ms. Both steps use the web CSS `ease` curve (R3). A toggle mid-way reverses from where the content is. Content built from a value that clears on close (a picked instrument, a selected bar) keeps its last value while it closes (web `CollapseOnExit` `lastChildrenRef`). The header keeps its own role and Expanded/Collapsed state; only the content moves. Under reduced motion the section opens and closes at once, with no height motion and no fade (R6). Each platform has one canonical accordion reveal that every consumer uses, so timing and sequence match everywhere. Owner (#561): "Accordion animated open then content fades in; when closing, content fades out then accordion collapses. This should happen anywhere there's an accordion style element in the app". This departs from the web `Accordion` (height only, 150 ms) by the owner's choice; the closing half matches web `CollapseOnExit`. MD3 motion: "Short 3 | 150ms | Small transitions" (recommendation). Windows keeps the native Fluent `Expander` (header, keyboard and UIA expand/collapse unchanged) and replaces only its content motion; Fluent Expander: "The Expander has a built-in animation for expanding and collapsing" (the built-in slide has no grow-then-fade sequence).
- **R11. A card fades in as one, with all its rows.** Owner-approved variant (#614, 2026-10-10), scoped to the native apps' cards that group several rows: on a page's first load, reload or page change, the card and every row in it start one 400 ms `fadeInUp` together; its rows never stagger inside it. Separate cards on a page may still stagger after one another (R3, R5). A card drawn as separately built segments (the Song leaderboard, Song band leaderboard and Full Rankings group card, [leaderboard-row](leaderboard-row.md) R10) takes the same fade on every segment, timed from the page's fade window, so a row the lazy list builds a moment later still starts with the others; a card built as one view fades as one view. Everything else in R5 holds: a scroll rushes the card's pending fade, a card built after the window has closed appears at once, a selected-row reveal waits for the card's entrance and the rows it realizes fade in with its rush, and a pinned selected row and pager keep their own reload behavior (R2). Under reduced motion the card shows at once (R6). Out of scope, keeping their own rules: pages whose rows are each their own card (Songs, Search results, Item Shop, Player History), the Paths table (R3), graph card lists (R7), accordions (R10), the First Run demo cascade (its demos replicate web `FirstRunCarousel`, not a load entrance) and the web, whose boards draw a card per row. Owner (#614): "Individual rows still stagger in on shared cards. App-wide issue and potentially multiplat. Native apps. (Title: all card content should fade in at once with card)". HIG Motion: "Add motion purposefully; gratuitous or excessive animation distracts and can cause physical discomfort." It departs from the web `PaginatedLeaderboard` row stagger by the owner's choice; Song Detail's previews, the Leaderboards overview, Compete, Rivals and Rival Detail cards already faded as one card on Apple.
- **R12. Paging a graph card's chart slides it to the new window.** A pager tap, a one-bar drag step or a swipe on a paged chart (rank history, score history) slides the bars, line and dates to the new window over 400 ms on the web CSS `ease` curve (web `CHART_ANIM_DURATION`; iOS `PlayerProfileCharts.move(to:)` animates the same page change), clipped to the plot, while the bars' value scale eases to the new window's axis; tick labels and the chart's accessibility description switch to the new window at once. A newer page change retargets the running slide from where it is. A resize, another chart or new data re-fits at once (iOS: the data-change animation is for paging, not for a measurement). The card, plot and date row keep their size, so nothing moves outside the plot. Under reduced motion the new window is drawn at once (R6). Agent decision (#593, 2026-10-10; owner may override): slide like iOS rather than morph the bars in place like the web's Recharts, because the owner asked for the iOS behavior and a slide shows which way the reader paged; timing and value axis follow the web. MD3 motion: "Medium 4 | 400ms" (recommendation).

- **R13. Centre page spinners in the visible result region, clear of an active fold (SFenton/festival-report-tracker#624).** The parent supplies the region below page controls and above lower chrome, inside system safe areas; the spinner never centres in scroll-content height or subtracts shell insets again. Apple page, reload, publication-refresh, Search, Notifications and split/secondary-pane indicators opt into `FestivalLoadingView(placement: .fill)`. It measures that container in global coordinates and reuses `FestivalEmptyState`'s `FoldAvoidingPlacement.region` with `DeviceLayout.splitHinge`: the larger usable side, leading/top on a tie, and the whole container when flat, folded or already beside the fold. The existing minimum-region policy stays unchanged. Cards, artwork, incremental footers and the already-separated Paths viewer keep intrinsic placement. This changes neither R2's single named busy indicator nor R3's timings. HIG `references/hig/layout.md`: "Respect system safe areas, margins, and guides"; `progress-indicators.md`: "Display progress indicators in a consistent location"; `designing-for-iphone-duo.md`: "use reserved-region APIs to keep important elements clear of the center" (should). Web `LoadGate.tsx` centres in `calc(100vh - Layout.shellChromeHeight)`. Evidence: hosted `FillingLoadingAccessibilityTests` and actual `SongDetailPageToolsGateTests.songDetailFirstLoadCentresItsNamedSpinnerOutsideTheFold` (phone, cover, flat, book, horizontal fold, offset pane; Large and AX5), plus the iPhone `GlobalSearchJourneyTests.testSearchingSpinnerIsCentredBelowScopeBar` (release CI: `apple-ci` `PLAIN_JOURNEYS`). Windows (#624): First-load and reload rings follow [empty-error-states](empty-error-states.md) R2: use the actual viewport below retained page controls and above lower chrome, not a fixed top offset or the height of scrolling content. Windows uses the native constrained `Grid` cell; a gate inside a page scroller uses its viewport-filling scroll presenter with an Auto header row and a star result row (Leaderboards `PageContent` / `ReadyRing`), so the loaded heading still scrolls with its cards. A board whose header or pager floats over (or scrolls inside) the result viewport wraps its ring in a stretching region that `LoadSwapVisual.PlaceSpinner` insets, only while loading, to the retained header's bottom and the footer's top (`LoadingRegion.Insets`, measured in the region's layout slot so its own margin never feeds back; the footer wins an overlap and the region never shrinks below the ring). Its region wrapper is Raw, non-hit-testable and holds a transparent fill so its UI Automation bounds are its layout box (journey `assertcentred`/`assertgap` are vacuous otherwise); only the named `ProgressRing` is a busy stop. The swap and its timings stay in `LoadSwapVisual` (R2-R3). Full-page and in-card rings are different regions: do not recenter a preview card's ring in the page. Separating-fold placement follows the platform's shared usable-region rule, never a screen midpoint on the hinge.
- **R14. Feedback form busy state is an owner-approved variant (Apple, #634).** The feedback form's sending status labels the spinner with a title and subtitle and crossfades only its text between upload and filing, with no fades under Reduce Motion; it uses this pattern's timings (300 ms content-out, the 150 ms spinner-in) and the one `FestivalLoadingView`. The rule lives in [modal-shell](modal-shell.md) R16; every page and region load keeps the unlabelled white spinner.

### Agent decision (SFenton/festival-report-tracker#624): extend the existing Apple placement policy

Chose shared filling placement over leaving the raw midpoint or introducing a spinner-only lower/right pane policy. It matches Apple's existing empty-state placement and web viewport-minus-chrome semantics without moving native chrome; HIG Duo recommends moving only what is necessary. The owner may override with `/choose`. Supersedes: Apple's full-region spinners previously had no active-fold placement; inline indicators and the empty-state policy are unchanged. Android's matching `LoadingRegion`/`HingeSide` fix is described under Canonical implementation; Windows' constrained-cell and `LoadingRegion.Insets` fix is described in R13.

## Canonical implementation

Page spinner placement extends [empty-error-states](empty-error-states.md) R2/R7 to loads (#624): centre in the visible content region below top/page controls and above bottom chrome, never in a fixed fraction of the viewport. On Android, `LoadingRegion` is shared by `LoadingView`, `FestivalLoadGate` and `LoadSwapSpinner`; it reads the measured region in window coordinates and applies the existing `HingeSide` policy (wider/leading book pane, lower tabletop pane, no split on a flat fold). A pane already beside the fold keeps its own centre. Gates whose content clears bottom chrome inside a scroll view pass `spinnerPadding`, so the spinner clears that chrome without shrinking the content viewport. Lazy-list/grid spinners use `rememberEmptyRegion`/`fillEmptyRegion`, including every board and Player Bands. Local in-card spinners and Paths keep their own region (`LoadSwapSpinner(avoidHinge = false)` for the Rank History card). This changes no fade timing or busy-indicator semantics (R2/R3/R6). Material 3 `layout-and-responsive.md`, Hinge-Aware Layouts: "Never place interactive content or critical information across the hinge area"; flat posture is one Medium/Expanded window. Tests: `LoadingPlacementUiTest`, `SongLoadingAccessibilityJourneyTest`.

Windows R5 rush and selected-row reveal (#323, 2026-10-07, agent decision; the owner may override). One `StaggerArm` per list (`FadeIn.Stagger`/`Restagger`) or page scroller (`FadeIn.BeginEntrance`: Song Detail, Player Profile) owns the window, the rush and the replay guard; pages never build a second one (guard `windows-stagger-arm`). **Question:** what fades below the first screen, and when does the automatic jump to a selected row start?

- A. Keep #260: rows past the first screen appear opaque and a scroll just closes the window.
- B. Rows past the first screen fade with the last staggered row (`FadeInTiming.TailDelay`, the Apple decision B), and any scroll while the entrance runs rushes every pending fade.
- C. Fade every row on its own stagger however far down it is (web `staggerDelay(…) ?? 0` caps it).

**Chose B,** for the Apple reasons above and because A is the bug. Mechanics:

- The first scroll movement (≥ 1 DIP) while the entrance runs (`StaggerArm.IsRunning`: the window, a scheduled fade, or a held tail) returns `ArmScroll.Rushed`. `FadeIn` then restarts every pending fade of that arm at delay 0 (`RushPending`). Rows realized during the next 400 ms fade at delay 0 with them. Fades that already started keep running.
- A scroll after the entrance has finished returns `ArmScroll.Closed`: nothing fades or replays. A Suggestions batch appended into the open load arm is kept and never rushed (`RushLimit`), so only newly generated cards fade.
- `FadeIn`'s scroll tracker listens to `ViewChanging`/`ViewChanged` only while `StaggerArm.WatchesScroll` (armed, not closed and still closing on scroll): once a scroll has closed the load it unsubscribes until the next `Stagger`/`Restagger` arm, because every scroll event's args are new WinRT objects whose GC pressure made scrolling stutter (#642, [platforms/windows.md](../platforms/windows.md#gotchas)). An appended batch never closes on scroll, so its arm needs no events.
- A batch appended after the load was rushed or closed starts at its own first row, never merged into the load (#532): `StaggerArm.Arm` merges only into a load whose window still runs and has not been rushed. If the load's 400 ms rush is still running, the batch keeps it but is excluded from it (`RushLimit` = the batch's start), so its new cards stagger and old cards never replay. On a slow host, merging there replayed cards 1 and 2 and pinned every later batch to start 0.
- Batch cards never replay inside their window, and a batch merges into an open batch only until one of that batch's cards has faded (#532, agent decision; the owner may override). `StaggerArm` records when each card's fade starts: a card realized again inside the window (recycled by a scroll back) keeps its original start, and returns no delay once that start has passed. A batch that arrives after the previous batch began to fade staggers from its own first row; merging it there pushed its new cards past the visible stagger count, so they never faded.
- A page that keeps its content hidden after it loads (Song Detail fades its spinner out for 500 ms before `BeginEntrance`) calls `FadeIn.HoldEntrance(scroller)` when the load lands (#532). `BeginEntrance` then measures the reader's scroll from that held offset, so a scroll during the hidden phase rushes the entrance as soon as it begins (`fade-rush rushed=0`, every `Enter` at delay 0). Without the hold, the entrance started un-rushed after a scroll on a slow host. Player Profile has no hidden phase and doesn't hold.
- `SelectedRowReveal.Start` waits `FadeInTiming.RevealWait` = the row's stagger (or the tail's) + 400 ms, so the row's own entrance has finished (web waits + 300 ms). It holds the tail for the jump (`ExpectAutomaticScroll`), then rushes it (`RushForAutomaticScroll`, 500 ms of realization) and centres the row (`StartBringIntoView`, ratio 0.5, instant; see the selected-row reveal below). It doesn't scroll if the reader scrolled, the board reloaded or the page left. With animations off it still waits for the row to be realized, then scrolls at once without the wait or the rush (R6; #493, #470).
- Repeater boards (song leaderboard, Full Rankings) centre through `SelectedRowReveal.CentreRow` (#571): `GetOrCreateElement`, then `UpdateLayout`, then `StartBringIntoView`, re-asked on each rendered frame (up to 30) until the row's centre lies inside the scroll viewport. A selected row past the realized first screen (rank 17 at 225% text) is created by the scroll itself and has no arranged position, so the request was a silent no-op: the board stayed at the top with rows 1–5 recycled, an empty band under the header (`preview-rows-row-eleven` compact text-225, 2 of 3 local runs before, 10 of 10 after). The `--perf-log` trace writes `fade-reveal-retry` for each re-ask; one is normal (the instant scroll lands a frame later). Never centre a repeater row with a bare `StartBringIntoView`.
- Song leaderboard: the reveal runs on a fresh visit from Song Details' player score (route `?page=N&navToPlayer=true`, web `leaderboardAtSelectedPlayer`) and after a page change. Song band leaderboard: a page change scrolls to the top and then reveals the selected band on the new page. Full Rankings: the pinned row's jump (#318) waits the same way, whatever invoked it.
- Every delayed fade belongs to an entrance (PR #358 review): `FadeIn.Play` is immediate-only, and a delayed fade is `FadeIn.Enter(owner, element, natural)`, scheduled on its owner's arm. A page's own entrance is a load arm on its scroller (`FadeIn.BeginEntrance`): Song Detail's header, sections and board cards, and Player Profile's title, Overview heading and grid. A board's pinned "your rank" row is a sibling of the list's scroller but enters with its rows (`FadeIn.Enter(list, pinned, PinnedRowReveal.RevealDelay)` on the song leaderboard, the song band leaderboard and Full Rankings), so the rush that starts the rows starts it too. Finding the fades by `ScrollViewer` ancestry missed the pinned rows, and Player Profile had no rush at all.

| B. **Hold tail rows for the expected scroll, then rush them (chosen)** | Rows past the first screen fade in together with the jump, including the destination; on a tall window the visible tail rows join that rush, 0.4 s later than the plain tail | HIG Motion: "As much as possible, let people cancel animations rather than wait for completion" (should) | Web `useStaggerRush`: the scroll restarts every entrance still at opacity 0 |
| C. Leave it (the review's finding) | The destination row arrives opaque | none | Contradicts R5 |

**Chose B:** it is the only option that keeps both web rules (wait for the row; rush whatever the scroll reaches). One scope is the authority; there are no page-level settle timers (the Solo board's `staggerSettled` is gone). Mechanics:
- `SelectedRowReveal` calls `expectAutomaticScroll()` on the page scope before it waits. While that expectation is open, a `.pastFirstScreen` entrance whose time comes is held (`holds(_:)`, `waitToStart(after:start:)`).
- As it scrolls, it calls `prepareForAutomaticScroll(lasting: 0.35)`. With fades held, or none waiting, this rushes at once; otherwise the scroll's movement rushes them. The rush window becomes the scroll plus one fade, so rows the scroll realizes fade at delay 0.
- A cancelled reveal (`endAutomaticScrollExpectation(_:)`, by token, so a stale reveal can't end a newer one) or a reset releases held rows.
- Under Reduce Motion nothing is held and the jump is instant (R6).
- It scrolls only once the content it scrolls among has its full height (#327). A reload gate inside the page's scroll view (the band board's, below its retained song header) grows the scroll content to the rows' height over its 400 ms reveal, and `scrollTo` stops at the content's end of the moment without following it as it grows. On a starved main actor that reveal ran for seconds, so the jump stopped at the 286.5 pt end of the half-grown page, several rows short of the destination, and stayed there. An animated `FestivalReloadGate` reveal therefore tells the page scope it found in its environment (`beginContentReveal()`, ended by the reveal's `withAnimation` completion), and `SelectedRowReveal` awaits `contentRevealed()` after the entrance wait, by condition and with no time cap. A gate outside the scroll view (the Solo board and Full Rankings, whose `List`/`ScrollView` the gate itself reveals) finds no scope and adds no wait. A reset of the scope keeps the count, because the gate's reveal re-arms it in the same update.

A catch-up that fails, or still reads an older publication, settles back to the existing paused notice instead of spinning forever. The reader can still pull or tap Retry. Saved filters, sort and search are view-model state, so they survive the refresh.

Mechanics:
- `presentation/PublicationCatchUp.kt` `PublicationCatchUp` watches `FestivalApi.publicationChanges`. While the page's shown publication lags the newest one it calls `RetryingLoader.catchUp()`, a plain re-read (no forced revalidation) that keeps content and keeps the last success on failure.
- `SongsViewModel` exposes `publicationRefreshing`; `SongsScreen` swaps on `rememberLoadSwap(…, ready = !publicationRefreshing)` and hides the pull indicator during the catch-up.
- `FirstRunHost` `rememberFirstRunDemoCatalog` re-reads the demo catalogue on a new publication.
- The publications themselves come from any adopting read, and from the receive-only live socket `data/PublicationLiveUpdates.kt` `PublicationLiveConnection`. That is one anonymous `wss://…/api/ws?publicationId=N` connection while the app is started. It sends no frames and no headers beyond OkHttp's own. It re-reads `/api/publication` on connect and on `publication_changed`, and reconnects with 1 s to 30 s backoff, like web `useAppWebSocket` and Apple `PublicationLiveUpdates`. It is off for fixture transports and loopback origins.
