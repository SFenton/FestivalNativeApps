# Quick Links

> **What:** the ordered per-page section navigator, including its entry point, jump ownership, icons, and accessible current state. **Read when:** adding section navigation to a page or changing a section list.

Status: **current**, 2026-10-10. Provenance: #6, #11, #12, #15, #286, #303, #313, #389, #392, #416, #545, #586, #587, #590, #605.

## Intent

Quick Links presents sections in page order and moves directly to the selected section without losing context. It uses the platform's native chooser while maintaining one shared ordering, landing, and current-section contract.

## Web source (behavior reference)

| Web | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `FortniteFestivalWeb/src/hooks/ui/usePageQuickLinks.ts` (`usePageQuickLinks`, `DEFAULT_QUICK_LINK_SCROLL_OFFSET = 32`) | Orders anchors, tracks the active section, and owns a jump through arrival. |
| `FortniteFestivalWeb/src/components/page/PageQuickLinks.tsx` (`PageQuickLinksRail`, `PageQuickLinksModal`) | Shows the ordered, current-marked section chooser. |

## Rules

1. **R1. One controller per scroll container.** Pages declare stable web-compatible section IDs and hand their ordered list, frames, jump, and active state to the canonical controller; do not build a second menu, sheet, rail, or tracker.
2. **R2. Keep on-page order.** Parent sections precede nested sections and every presentation preserves that order. Apple menus must use `.menuOrder(.fixed)` (#6, #11). Every Quick Links page has a test comparing the rendered section order with the open chooser; on Android these are `QuickLinksPageOrderUiTest` and `QuickLinksPageSweepUiTest`, and Songs is covered for every bucketed sort (#158).
3. **R3. Hide inert navigation.** Show an entry point only with at least two current sections; remove unavailable sections rather than offering a no-op choice. The entry also waits for the content it jumps through (load-transition R1): a page whose sections exist before their content loads hides it until the first reveal (Windows `QuickLinksViewModel.IsContentShown`, #649). **Owner-approved variant (#545, Rivalry):** a page that is one flat list of entries, such as Rivalry (a Rival Detail category's View All), has no sections, so it offers no Quick Links at all. A chooser with one entry per row only repeats the list. See the variant below.
4. **R4. Land and mark together.** Use the shared 32-unit landing/active line and jump ownership from [section-jump-landing](section-jump-landing.md); selected items remain current through arrival and near-end clamping.
5. **R5. Use native presentation.** **Approved variants:** Apple uses the tab-bar accessory sheet or toolbar menu (#92 follow-up); Android uses a compact bottom sheet and wider dropdown menu (operator 2026-09-28); Windows uses `QuickLinksMenuButton` or a wide pane; crossing that breakpoint closes the collapsed side's tooltips and moves its keyboard focus to the other side programmatically (`Controls/CollapsedToolTip`, #571; [platforms/windows.md](../platforms/windows.md#gotchas)). Material 3: "Material Android is Compose-first."
6. **R6. Preserve accessibility and hit targets.** The entry exposes the current section; the selected row has a current/selected state; rows have names and section jumps announce their destination. Apple touch controls meet HIG Accessibility's 44x44 pt default minimum. An Apple menu row's spoken name sits on its title `Text` too (`QuickLinkLabel`): iOS 27 menus read only that text and drop the row's own label and identifier, so the Duo/iPad menu would read "Rank History" for "Lead Rank History" (#626; hosted `quickLinksRowTitleTextReadsTheSpokenTitle`; journeys find iOS 27 menu rows by that exact name when the identifier is gone, `QuickLinksIconAccessibilityJourneyTests`); Android menu actions use 48 dp; Windows page tools, their menu items and the pane rows use 40 epx (menu items through the app-wide `FSTMenuItemMinHeight` style, [page-tools-and-nav-chrome](page-tools-and-nav-chrome.md) R10, #416). Tests: Apple hosted `QuickLinksAccessibilityTests` (in `apple-ci`: chooser and entry (#389); rotor order on Compete, Song Detail and Profile, nested rows, wrapping (#392)) and the iPhone journeys `QuickLinksAccessibilityJourneyTests` (Settings sheet audit, #389) and `QuickLinksOrderJourneyTests.testQuickLinksSheetIsAccessible*` (nested Profile sheet, hit size, AX5 audit, #392); Windows `a11y-quick-links-landing.json` (in `windows-ui`: names, order, 40 epx items and rows, landings, #416) and `HitTargetMarkupTests`; Android `@DeviceCi` ATF journey `ProfileQuickLinksAccessibilityJourneyTest` (in `android-device`: the Profile entry's name, role and 48 dp target; chooser rows in page order on screen and as TalkBack reads them, the current row's selected state; the > 8-section sheet opening fully expanded with no Expand action (`modal-shell` R13, #586) and its lower rows reached by scrolling the list; the Top Songs jump landing and holding 32 dp below the bar at 1.0x and 2.0x; and the closed shell drawer out of the reading order, #501) and `SongsAccessibilityJourneyTest#songDetailQuickLinksJump*` (Song Detail's Bass jump lands the Bass heading, not its padding, on the 32 dp line at 1.0x and 2.0x, the entry then names Bass and TalkBack reads the heading after it, #587).
7. **R7. Normalize artwork icons.** Instrument artwork is pre-sized so its *visible* disc matches the adjacent system symbols, and scales with text; it never renders at its 144 pt intrinsic size (#303). The full-bleed artwork's dark outer ring disappears on dark surfaces, so an equal frame reads smaller than the symbols: Apple sizes per chooser (`QuickLinkLabel.Presentation`): 24 pt in the iPhone sheet list beside 20 pt symbols, 17 pt in iOS menus (iPad, Duo) beside 13.5 pt menu symbols, and 16 pt in macOS menus. It follows the row symbols' Dynamic Type curve, which holds from xxxLarge to AX3 while text keeps growing (#313). HIG Icons: "Adjust dimensions for visual weight so they look consistent, rather than forcing equal geometry."
8. **R8. Owner-approved: no Quick Links on Rivalry (owner, #545).** The Rivalry page (a Rival Detail category's View All, web `RivalryPage`) offers no Quick Links on the native apps: one link per song only repeats the list. Sections stay the unit of navigation, so the other Rivals pages keep theirs (Rivals hub and Leaderboard Rivals cards, Rival Detail categories, Compete groups). See [the variant](#owner-approved-variant-no-quick-links-on-rivalry-545).
9. **R9. Every Rival Detail category has its own icon (owner, #605).** Rival Detail's chooser gives each category row a distinct system icon from one shared category list, so the group has icons on every row (HIG Menus: "give every item an icon, or give none of them icons"). The web `RivalDetailPage` has no icons there; this native extension is the owner's ask. Icons stay decorative: rows speak only the category name. Unknown keys fall back to the Rivals people icon, so the group stays uniform. Apple uses outline glyphs so the set keeps one stroke weight beside the thin arrows (HIG Icons: "same size, detail level, stroke weight"; `forward.fill`/`crown.fill` read heavier on iPhone). Each platform keeps its system set and maps the same keys:

   | Key | Title | Meaning | Apple (SF Symbol) | Android (Material Symbol, suggested) | Windows (Segoe Fluent) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | `closest_battles` | Closest Battles | neck and neck | `arrow.left.arrow.right` | `compare_arrows` | nearest glyph, own lane |
   | `almost_passed` | Almost Passed | closing in | `arrow.up.right` | `trending_up` | nearest glyph, own lane |
   | `slipping_away` | Slipping Away | falling behind | `arrow.down.right` | `trending_down` | nearest glyph, own lane |
   | `barely_winning` | Barely Winning | narrow win | `flag.checkered` | `sports_score` | nearest glyph, own lane |
   | `pulling_forward` | Pulling Forward | growing lead | `forward` | `fast_forward` | nearest glyph, own lane |
   | `dominating_them` | Dominating Them | commanding lead | `crown` | `crown` (or `military_tech`) | nearest glyph, own lane |

   Apple: `RivalCategorization.quickLinkSymbol(forKey:)` / `RivalCategory.quickLinkSection` (`FestivalCore/Rivals.swift`), used by `RivalDetailScreen` on iPhone, iPad, Duo and Mac. Tests: `RivalsTests.rivalCategoryQuickLinkSymbolsAreDistinctPerCategory` and hosted `QuickLinksAccessibilityTests.rivalDetailCategoryRowsHaveDistinctDecorativeIcons` (distinct real symbols; rows read only the names in order as full-width buttons; no image elements). Android and Windows map the same keys below the canonical implementation table.
10. **R10. Nested sections land on their card, inside a parent row (#590).** A nested section (depth 1) may be a card inside its parent's list or grid item rather than an item of its own. The player page's Quick Links match iOS: Global Statistics; per Settings-visible chart its entry, then nested "Rank History" (`rank-history:<wire>`, trend icon, while the chart's Rank History card is on the page: played, and loading, failed or loaded with snapshots) and "Percentiles" (`percentiles:<wire>`, stacked-bar icon, when the chart has a percentile table), each spoken "<Chart> Rank History" / "<Chart> Percentiles"; then Top Songs and Bands (Android keeps Top Songs, which iOS's page lacks). Android: `ProfileSections.quickLinks`/`rowKey`/`isNested` (`core/profile/ProfileLayout.kt`); a nested card reports its offset in its row with `Modifier.quickLinkAnchor(QuickLinkAnchors, id)`, and `rememberQuickLinks(offsetOf = …)` adds it to both the jump and the tracked frame (a nested section with no measured offset is skipped by the tracker). The Android menu indents nested rows 16 dp per depth like the sheet. Tests: `ProfileParityCoreTest.quickLinksNestRankHistoryAndPercentilesUnderTheirChartLikeIos`, `ProfileParityUiTest.nestedQuickLinksLandOnRankHistoryAndPercentiles` and the ATF journey `ProfileQuickLinksAccessibilityJourneyTest` (nested rows under their chart, read in order).

### Owner-approved variant: no Quick Links on Rivalry (#545)

Owner (#545): "Some Rivals subpages have QL that have entries for each song. Those pages don't need ql". The web `pages/rivals/RivalryPage.tsx` builds one mobile Quick Link per song (`rivalrySongQuickLinkId`). The native Rivalry pages drop them: no entry point (toolbar menu, iPhone tab-bar accessory, Android action, Windows pane), no "Quick Links" rotor and no Go-menu section commands. Do not restore per-song links to match the web, and do not apply this variant to pages whose Quick Links jump to real sections.

- **Scope.** Only Rivalry (`/rivals/:rivalId/rivalry?mode=`). Rivals pages whose Quick Links jump to real sections keep the base rules: the Rivals hub cards, Rival Detail's categories (with R9 icons), Compete groups and the Leaderboards Rivals tab. All Rivals already had none. The web is unchanged.
- **Apple (2026-10-09).** `Features/Rivals/RivalryScreen.swift` has no `QuickLinksController`, `.quickLinks` container or `.quickLinkSection` rows, so iPhone, iPad, Mac and Duo show only View Profile. Guards: hosted `RivalsRenderTests.rivalryOffersViewProfileButNoQuickLinks` (rows read in order, no rotor or entry, the accessory reads View Profile then Notifications as 44 pt buttons) and `rivalDetailKeepsCategoryQuickLinks`; iPhone journey `QuickLinksOrderJourneyTests.testRivalryOffersNoQuickLinks`.
- **Android (2026-10-09).** `RivalryScreen` (`ui/rivals/RivalPages.kt`) builds no `QuickLinksController`; its top-app-bar actions are Sort, then View Profile. The per-song builder (`RivalQuickLinks.rivalry`/`songId`) is gone. Guards: `QuickLinksPageSweepUiTest.rivalryHasNoQuickLinksOnPhone`/`rivalryHasNoQuickLinksInWideWindow`, `RivalsUiTest.rivalryOffersNoQuickLinks`, and the ATF journey `PlayerAccessibilityJourneyTest.rivalsHubDetailAndRivalry` (Rival Detail keeps the entry; Rivalry's TalkBack order has Sort, then View Profile, no Quick Links stop; 48 dp targets).
- **Windows.** `RivalryPage` never had Quick Links (the precedent).

| Sub-behavior | Apple | Android | Windows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordered model and tracker | `apple/Sources/FestivalCore/QuickLinks.swift` `QuickLinks` | `android/app/src/main/java/com/festivalscoretracker/android/core/quicklinks/QuickLinks.kt` `QuickLinks` | `windows/Festival.Core/Domain/QuickLinks.cs` `QuickLinks` |

| Native chooser | `apple/Sources/FestivalUI/Common/QuickLinks/QuickLinksToolbar.swift` `QuickLinksMenu` | `android/app/src/main/java/com/festivalscoretracker/android/ui/quicklinks/QuickLinksUi.kt` `QuickLinksAction` | `windows/Festival.App/Controls/QuickLinksMenuButton.cs` `QuickLinksMenuButton` |

**Android adopts R9 (2026-10-10, #605).** `RivalQuickLinks.categoryIcon` (`core/rivals/RivalQuickLinks.kt`) maps each category key to an icon token and `RivalQuickLinks.rivalDetail` puts it on every row; the canonical `quickLinkIcon` (`ui/quicklinks/QuickLinksUi.kt`) maps the tokens to outlined Material icons: `compare` → `AutoMirrored.Outlined.CompareArrows`, `trending-up`/`trending-down` → `AutoMirrored.Outlined.TrendingUp`/`TrendingDown`, `finish-flag` → `SportsScore`, `fast-forward` → `FastForward`, `medal` → `MilitaryTech` for Dominating Them (the Compose icon set has no `crown`). An unknown key gets `people`. The icon renders with `contentDescription = null` and only the `fst.quick-links.icon` test tag (no label or role), so rows read only the category name. Tests: `RivalsCoreTest.rivalDetailCategoriesHaveDistinctIcons` (the shared list, distinct glyphs, none the generic `Toc` or another Rivals icon); the render test `RivalDetailQuickLinkIconsUiTest` (phone sheet and wide menu, drawn in software: each row's icon pixels best match its assigned glyph rasterised from the `ImageVector` paths, ahead of the other category glyphs, `Toc` and `people`, and the rows differ from each other, so an absent, generic or swapped icon fails); and the ATF journeys `PlayerAccessibilityJourneyTest.rivalDetailQuickLinksIconsAreDecorative` and `…AtDoubleText` (rows in page order, named only by their category, one sized icon node per row with no image role or label, 48 dp targets, 1.0x and 2.0x text).

### Windows R9 glyphs (#605)

`RivalCategorization.QuickLinkGlyph(key)` / `RivalCategoryItem.QuickLinkSection` (`windows/Festival.Core/Domain/Rivals.cs`, `ViewModels/RivalDetailViewModels.cs`) map R9's keys to `SymbolThemeFontFamily` glyphs: `closest_battles` Switch U+E8AB, `almost_passed` Market U+EAFC, `slipping_away` Rewind U+EB9E, `barely_winning` Flag U+E7C1, `pulling_forward` FastForward U+EB9D, `dominating_them` FavoriteStar U+E734 (Segoe has no crown); unknown keys fall back to the Rivals hub's People U+E716. The font resolves to Segoe MDL2 Assets on Windows 10 (the app's minimum), so every glyph must exist in MDL2 too: Fluent-only glyphs such as MarketDown U+EF42 render as tofu there. `QuickLinksMenuButton` draws them as decorative `FontIcon`s that UIA never exposes; rows keep their names. Tests: `RivalsCoreTests.QuickLinkGlyph_IsDistinctPerCategory`, `RivalsViewModelTests.RivalDetail_CategorizesAndLinksRivalry` and the `a11y-narration.json` page `narr-quick-links-rival-detail-menu` (names, toggle state, order, 40 epx rows and a glyph painted just before each row's text).
