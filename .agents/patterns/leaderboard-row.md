# Leaderboard row

> **What:** shared score and ranking row geometry, columns, name overflow, pager and selected-player behavior. **Read when:** adding or changing any leaderboard, score preview, ranking card, spotlight or board footer.

Status: **current**, 2026-10-06. Provenance: #37, #38, #90, #170, #172, #185, #188, #264, #292, #293, #294, #295, #306, #307, #318, #319.

## Intent

Every row in one leaderboard section must read as one aligned, accessible data table: a stable minimum height, a shared column plan, a contained name, and the same pager and pinned-row behavior regardless of entry point.

## Rules

- **R1. Fit columns once per section.** Rank, score, rating and songs widths include spotlight and pinned rows; individual rows never choose their own plan. Use measured row/card width, not device identity. Score sections show the season from a 520-unit row and stars from 700 by width alone (web `resolveTopScoresColumns`); when those columns don't fit on one line beside a minimum name (large text), the section's rows stack (R2) instead of dropping them (#170). A measured width outlives navigation: a page that returns from the back stack draws its first frame with the plan it left with, never an unmeasured plan that drops, adds or restacks a column for a frame (#185). HIG Layout: "Choose layout from size classes, not device type/idiom or orientation."
- **R2. Use a 48-unit minimum.** Loaded rows, selected and pinned rows, skeletons and spotlight placeholders share the web baseline; accessibility layouts may grow and stack. A compact second line in the value column (the percentile metrics' small Bayesian value) fits inside the 48 units rather than growing the row, and where large text stacks the loaded rows, the skeleton takes the same stacked lines so the rows still don't jump (Android, #188). Stacked large-text rows reserve a fixed name budget that the skeleton reserves too, so a wrapped name neither makes its row taller than its neighbours nor the card jump; the budget is a minimum, never a cap, so a longer name still wraps rather than being cut off (R3). HIG Layout: "rows/containers may grow to avoid clipping/overlap and allow multiple lines."
- **R3. Keep names inside their column.** Overflowing normal-size names marquee through the shared text component; Reduce Motion, inactive/off-screen rows and motion-disabled systems tail-truncate, while accessibility text wraps and assistive technology receives the full name. HIG Motion: "When Reduce Motion is on, reduce automatic and repetitive animation."
- **R4. Decide Compete songs once per card.** Hide songs played/total on every row only when the longest drawn name cannot fit; keep it in the accessibility value. Other ranking sections keep their shared songs column.
- **R5. Reuse the board pager.** Full, band and song boards share one pager layout and button semantics; the selected player's pinned row is immediately above it and shares its columns. The pager's arrows and `page / total` badge draw on the same card surface as the rows beside them (fill, translucency, rim and contrast fallback; [surface-materials](surface-materials.md) R1), never a pager-only plate; rows fade out above the pager and are not drawn beneath it ([scroll-edge](scroll-edge.md)), which keeps its glyphs legible (#319). Web `Paginator` spreads `frostedCard` on its arrows, the rows' surface. The pager stays in the content under its board in every layout, including Apple's Duo vertical-bar poses and a split's trailing pane, never as toolbar or rail items; unfolded, it sits on the screen beside the vertical bar rather than across the hinge (#345). HIG iPhone Duo: "Keep controls near the content they affect."
- **R7. One selected-row rule for players and bands (#307, #318).** Solo and band song boards and the full instrument rankings board pin the selected profile's row above the pager on every page, the page that already shows the row included, with the same row component (web `FullRankingsPage` `hasPlayerFooter = !!playerRanking`; Android decides it with `RankingSpotlight.pinnedPlacement`, the overview cards with `RankingSpotlight.placement`). Only the Leaderboards overview cards hide a footer for a player already in their top rows (web `RankingCard`). A selected row shown apart from its page (Song Detail's appended row, a footer while the row is on another page) jumps to the page containing its rank and reveals the highlighted row once its own entrance has finished, rushing the rest of the page's stagger ([load-transition](load-transition.md) R5, #323; instant under Reduce Motion); once the row is on screen it opens the profile (Statistics for a player, the Band page for a band). Other rows open their player or band. Labels name the destination ("Jump to your band's position" / "Open band"). HIG Lists and tables: "Provide appropriate selection feedback."
- **R8. Stack score footers once per section, never truncate the score (#264).** A row's team score footer (web `SongBandScoreFooter`: score, then FC, accuracy and stars) keeps the score whole at every width and text size. When any realized row in a section (one Song Detail band preview card, one song band board) measures that its badges don't fit beside its score, every row in that section puts the badges on a line under the score; the plan is a measured section-wide decision (R1), made at measure time so virtualized rows report the stacked height at once, and rows grow (R2). Rows report only their own need, so the plan converges. Badges may clip at their own line's edge only at extreme sizes; the score never does. A new footer reuses the platform's canonical footer below instead of its own grid, wrap or trimming. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize text); HIG Layout: "rows/containers may grow to avoid clipping/overlap and allow multiple lines."
- **R6. Maintain platform-approved variants.** Android and Windows use their native shared pager and row surface; their title/header behavior is not an Apple collapsing-title requirement. MD3 transitions and Fluent theme brushes are approved native variants (#294).

### Agent decision: the pager never moves into the rail (#345)

Agent decision (#345, 2026-10-07, owner may override with `/choose`): on iPhone Duo every paged board (Song Leaderboard, Song Band Leaderboard, Full Rankings, Band Rankings, Player Bands) shows the shared in-content `RankingsPagerView` under the board in every pose, with the selected row pinned above it, as the owner asked. The rail's First/Previous/Next/Last toolbar items (`RankingsPagerToolbarContent`, `/duo` B2/D5) are gone. Unfolded, `PagerScreenPlacement` confines the pager to the screen beside the vertical bar (the right screen with a trailing bar) when that screen is at least 320 pt wide, otherwise the other screen, otherwise the centre; a split's trailing pane already lies on one screen and keeps its centred pager. Band Rankings' floating bar shows only the pager there; its board switcher stays in the rail. Guarded by hosted renders with an injected vertical-bar `\.deviceLayout` (`DuoBoardPagerHostedTests`): Band Rankings' `RankingsFloatingBar` and the Solo board in each Duo pose and in a split's trailing pane must realize all five pager controls under the board, clear of the hinge unfolded, with the selected row's footer directly above; restoring an empty vertical-bar branch fails them.

| Option | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A. Keep the rail pager on vertical-bar poses | No pager under the board; paging is a rail chevron far from the rows. | Owner rejected it (#345). |
| **B. In-content pager, beside the vertical bar when unfolded (chosen)** | One pager under the board everywhere; unfolded it sits next to the bar for quicker reach and clear of the hinge. | Owner quote; HIG iPhone Duo: "Keep controls near the content they affect" and "use reserved-region APIs to keep important elements clear of the center" (strong); Android's unfolded song band board puts its pager on the trailing side. |
| C. In-content pager centred across both screens | The arrows and badge straddle the hinge. | HIG iPhone Duo hinge guidance (strong). |

### Agent decision: skeletons drawn by the canonical row (#281)

Agent decision (#281, 2026-10-06, owner may override with `/choose`): Windows draws the Leaderboards overview skeleton and the spotlight's "loading your rank" row with the canonical `LeaderboardEntryRow`, fed a `LeaderboardSkeletonRow` whose invisible text sits in the same column plan as the loaded rows. Rank, name and rating bars (plus a songs bar when large text puts the label on its own line) are drawn in those cells. The skeleton's section is the widest plausible top-ten content for the card's metric (`#10`, `999 / 999`, `999,999,999`, `Top 0.99%` or `100.0%`), and a percentile metric adds the Bayesian line. The spotlight placeholder fits the board's measured section and draws no bars under its ring. Measured before (live, 500-epx window at 200% text): the skeleton stayed 48 epx while loaded Total Score rows were 66.7 and Adjusted rows 97.7, so cards jumped when they loaded. After: `tools/windows/journeys/leaderboard-skeleton.json` asserts the skeleton row pitch equals the loaded pitch at compact, medium and wide sizes, at 100% and 200% text.

| Option | Result | Guidance and precedent |
|---|---|---|
| A. Fixed 48-epx bars (before, #90) | Correct at 100% text; at large text or under a percentile metric the cards jump when rows arrive. | Breaks R2's no-jump rule. |
| B. Bars sized from `TextScaleLayout.Factor` | Height approximates the loaded rows, but it is a second row metric that drifts from the real plan (stacking depends on width too). | Pattern-first: no parallel row metric. |
| **C. Placeholder rows through the canonical row (chosen)** | The skeleton and the loaded rows share one plan, padding and minimum height at every width, text size and metric. | R2; Android `RankingsSkeletonRows` (#188); winui-design `layout-review.md`: "Loading — progress text or skeleton", "Pick the lightest change that preserves the task… Reflow". |

The placeholder rows are raw-view only (Narrator skips them, as it skipped the old bars) and are not focusable. The ring keeps its "Loading your rank" name.

### Agent decision: Android large-text name budget (#188)

Agent decision (#188, 2026-10-06, owner may override with `/choose`): at large text (font scale 1.3 and up), stacked ranking rows on every surface (Leaderboards cards, Full Rankings, Band Rankings, Compete) reserve a fixed number of name lines with `minLines` and no line cap: two for players (`STACKED_RANKING_NAME_LINES`; Epic display names are at most 16 characters, which wrap to at most two lines at 2.0) and one per member for bands (`stackedBandNameLines`: Duos 2, Trios 3, Quads 4). `RankingsSkeletonRows` reserves the same budget, so the Leaderboards cards no longer grow when a wrapped name arrives (FST_Resizable desktop 2.0: a wrapped name made its row 180 vs 138 dp). Narrow stacked rows at normal text (#115, #116) have no skeleton and keep names at their own length.

| Option | Result | Guidance and precedent |
|---|---|---|
| A. One-line name budget (before) | Short names stay compact; a wrapped name grows its row and the card jumps after loading. | Breaks R2's no-jump rule. |
| **B. Fixed multi-line budget as a minimum (chosen)** | Rows in a section share one height and match the skeleton; one-line names leave a blank line at 2.0; rosters longer than the budget still grow. | R2 (rows don't jump), R3 (accessibility text wraps); MD3 Lists come in fixed "One-line, Two-line, Three-line" variants (consider); WCAG 1.4.4. |
| C. Cap names at the budget with an ellipsis | Fully deterministic heights. | R3 and WCAG 1.4.4: large text must wrap, not truncate. |

B wins: it is the only option that keeps both R2 and R3. Web rows never stack, so there is no web precedent; the trade-off is extra height per row at large text. Residual, accepted: a single member name wider than the whole name column (on FST_Phone at 2.0, about 15 wide characters) still wraps onto a line beyond the budget (one live Trios row of five, 4 lines in a 3-line budget). Reserving that worst case would double the blank space in every row, and one roster per line cannot help, because wrapping the joined roster never takes more lines than breaking it per member.

### Agent decision: Android selected rows (#307)

Agent decision (2026-10-06, owner may override with `/choose`): Android pins the selected player's and band's rows on the solo and band song boards whenever the board returns them, as on the web and Apple, instead of hiding the footer while the row is on screen; the band board moved onto the shared `RankingsBoardLayout` so both footers sit above one pager, and the band footer reuses `SelectedScoreFooterRow` through `SongBandLeaderboardEntry.footerLeaderboardEntry`. Routes carry `navToPlayer` / `navToBand` with the page, and the reveal centres the row above the footer, instantly under Reduce Motion. Rejected: keeping hide-when-visible footers (a second rule beside web and Apple) and an in-list band footer (diverges from the solo board). MD3 Lists: "In Compose, prefer Material3 list patterns"; touch targets stay 48dp and TalkBack click labels name the destination.

### Agent decision: the pinned row is the jump control (#307)

Agent decision (2026-10-06, owner may override with `/choose`): on the Windows solo and band song boards the pinned row itself jumps or opens per R7, and the separate **Your Page** button above the solo board's pinned row is gone. Options weighed: A) the pinned row is the control (chosen); B) keep **Your Page** on the solo board and add one to the band board; C) both. A follows the web (PR #168) and Apple, and Fluent ListView guidance gives each item one invoke action, so a second button that did the row's job added a Tab stop without adding a destination. Windows Full Rankings made the same change in #318 (`LeaderboardsSpotlight`'s pinned row runs `SelectedRowAction.Footer`), so no Windows board keeps a Your Page button.

### Agent decision: Android band board columns (#172)

Agent decision (2026-10-06, owner may override with `/choose`): on the full song band board, the member cards adopt the pinned footer row's geometry instead of the footer adopting the cards'. `bandBoardColumns` builds one plan from the page rows and the pinned band (rank width measured in both rank styles), fits the footer's `SelectedScoreFooterRow` with it (`rememberScoreColumns(minRankWidth)`) and starts the cards' rank at the footer's 20 dp inset with the footer's gap, so an off-page "#9,968" lines up with the page (R1, R5). R7 keeps the solo-style footer row, which the web also uses (`SongBandLeaderboardPage` renders `LeaderboardEntry` in its footer, although it sizes that rank column separately). Rejected: drawing the footer as a full member card (a four-member card pinned over the list costs most of a phone screen at 2.0 text and breaks R7), and shrinking the footer's insets to the card's 12 dp (the purple highlight would touch the card edge). Song Detail's band cards keep their 12 dp inset and 10 dp gap, as Song Detail's solo cards keep theirs. `StackedScoreRow` puts its 8 dp gap (`STACKED_RANK_GAP`) outside the shared rank width (as #149 also found), so names start at the same x on every large-text row. MD3 Lists: "In Compose, prefer Material3 list patterns".

### Agent decision: score rows stack instead of dropping the season (#170)

Agent decision (#170, 2026-10-05, owner may override with `/choose`): Android score sections (Song Detail instrument cards, the song board and its pinned rows, the band board footer) keep the season from a 520 dp row and stars from 700 dp, as the web's `resolveTopScoresColumns` does (`showSeason: width >= 520`, `showStars: width >= MOBILE_BREAKPOINT`, no fit exception). When those columns plus a minimum name don't fit on one line, which in practice happens only at large text, `LeaderboardColumnLayout.fit` sets `stacked` for the whole section and `ScoreRow` draws its existing `StackedScoreRow` (rank and wrapping name, then score, accuracy, season and stars in a `FlowRow`). Rows without a season or stars never stack for fit, so narrow phones are unchanged.

| Option | Result | Guidance and precedent |
|---|---|---|
| A. Keep the fit-based drop (stars, then season) | 520–640 dp cards at 200% hide the season, unlike the web. | A native copy with no web precedent. |
| **B. Stack the rows and keep the columns (chosen)** | Every row at least 520 dp shows the season at every text size; the rows grow taller. | Web `resolveTopScoresColumns` (width only); R2 "accessibility layouts may grow and stack"; WCAG 1.4.4 Resize text; MD3 Lists: "In Compose, prefer Material3 list patterns" (consider). |
| C. Keep the season and squeeze the name | The name marquees in a sliver of the row at 200%. | R3 wants names readable, and accessibility text wraps. |

B wins under the precedence table: web behavior beats an undocumented native copy, and it reuses the large-text layout that already exists instead of adding a new one. Windows' `LeaderboardColumnLayout.Fit` still drops the season on fit (see Known debt). Apple's `LeaderboardRowColumns` decides it from width alone.
