# Leaderboard row

> **What:** shared score and ranking row geometry, columns, name overflow, pager and selected-player behavior. **Read when:** adding or changing any leaderboard, score preview, ranking card, spotlight or board footer.

Status: **current**, 2026-10-10. Provenance: #37, #38, #90, #170, #172, #185, #188, #264, #292, #293, #294, #295, #306, #307, #318, #319, #352, #353, #361, #364, #370, #455, #543, #594.

## Intent

Every row in one leaderboard section must read as one aligned, accessible data table: a stable minimum height, a shared column plan, a contained name, and the same pager and pinned-row behavior regardless of entry point.

## Rules

- **R1. Fit columns once per section.** Rank, score, rating and songs widths include spotlight and pinned rows; individual rows never choose their own plan. Use measured row/card width, not device identity. Score sections show the season from a 520-unit row and stars from 700 by width alone (web `resolveTopScoresColumns`); when those columns don't fit on one line beside a minimum name (large text), the section's rows stack (R2) instead of dropping them (#170). A measured width outlives navigation: a page that returns from the back stack draws its first frame with the plan it left with, never an unmeasured plan that drops, adds or restacks a column for a frame (#185). Score History lists are sections too: one score width from the section's longest score, and a row without accuracy keeps the accuracy column's space, so scores and season pills line up (web `scoreWidth`, #594). HIG Layout: "Choose layout from size classes, not device type/idiom or orientation."
- **R2. Use a 48-unit minimum.** Loaded rows, selected and pinned rows, skeletons and spotlight placeholders share the web baseline; accessibility layouts may grow and stack. A compact second line in the value column (the percentile metrics' small Bayesian value) fits inside the 48 units rather than growing the row, and where large text stacks the loaded rows, the skeleton takes the same stacked lines so the rows still don't jump (Android, #188). Stacked large-text rows reserve a fixed name budget that the skeleton reserves too, so a wrapped name neither makes its row taller than its neighbours nor the card jump; the budget is a minimum, never a cap, so a longer name still wraps rather than being cut off (R3). HIG Layout: "rows/containers may grow to avoid clipping/overlap and allow multiple lines." A stacked row still fills the row's full width, like a one-line row whose name column takes the spare space: its target and the selected player's band span the card, never just its text (Apple `RankingRowLayout`, #493: at AX5 a Compete card's rows had ended at their own text, 331 against 357 pt; guards `CompeteYourRankAccessibilityTests.stackedRowsFillTheCardWhenTheirValuesWrap`, and for Song Details' grouped card `GroupedCardAccessibilityTests.stackedRankingRowsFillTheGroupCard` with the AX5 `GroupedCardAccessibilityJourneyTests` journey, #499).
- **R3. Keep names inside their column.** Overflowing normal-size names marquee through the shared text component; Reduce Motion, inactive/off-screen rows and motion-disabled systems tail-truncate, while accessibility text wraps and assistive technology receives the full name. HIG Motion: "When Reduce Motion is on, reduce automatic and repetitive animation." **Owner-approved variant (#364, song boards in a split's trailing pane on Apple):** when any name in the section would marquee, every row and the pinned row become a multi-row card with the full name on its own line (see below). Full-width, pushed and iPhone boards keep the marquee.
- **R4. Decide Compete songs once per card.** Hide songs played/total on every row only when the longest drawn name cannot fit; keep it in the accessibility value. Other ranking sections keep their shared songs column, except where a platform already fits them: Apple Full Rankings (`/duo` J2) and Leaderboards overview cards (#352), Android overview cards (name under 32 dp).
- **R5. Reuse the board pager.** Full, band and song boards share one pager layout and button semantics; it shows only once a loaded board has given a page count above one, never as a placeholder "1 / 1" ([load-transition](load-transition.md) R4, #575), and on Windows it hides from an in-place board switch until the new board's count commits (load-transition R4, agent decision #575); the selected player's pinned row is immediately above it and shares its columns. On Android the pinned row and pager rest one standard 16 dp gap above the bottom chrome actually shown (the floating toolbar while the page has tools, else the bottom bar), never where an absent pinned row or toolbar would sit (#578, `RankingsBoardScaffold` anchors them at `FestivalScreen`'s padding with no extra margin; the rows end one item gap above them, #293). The pager's arrows and `page / total` badge draw on the same card surface as the rows beside them (fill, translucency, rim and contrast fallback; [surface-materials](surface-materials.md) R1), never a pager-only plate; rows fade out above the pager and are not drawn beneath it ([scroll-edge](scroll-edge.md)), which keeps its glyphs legible (#319). Screen readers reach the board's header and rows first, then the pinned row, then the pager left to right, as the web's DOM order reads (#461; Apple's bottom safe-area inset otherwise read the chrome first on the song band board). A row that takes keyboard or screen-reader focus scrolls clear of the pinned row and pager, never under them (WCAG 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured; Windows `BoardFooter.Inset` for every board, #409, including the centred selected-row jump, #461; Android clips rows at the footer's top so covered rows leave TalkBack, #104). Web `Paginator` spreads `frostedCard` on its arrows, the rows' surface. The pager stays in the content under its board in every layout, including Apple's Duo vertical-bar poses and a split's trailing pane, never as toolbar or rail items; unfolded, it sits on the screen beside the vertical bar rather than across the hinge in book pose or the columns' midpoint when flat (#345, #361). HIG iPhone Duo: "Keep controls near the content they affect." When a full-width board pairs its rows in wide landscape ([wide-columns](wide-columns.md) R8, #353), the pinned row and the pager stay one full-width bar under both columns, and each column fits the section's one plan (R1) at the column's width.
- **R6. Maintain platform-approved variants.** Android and Windows use their native shared pager and row surface; their title/header behavior is not an Apple collapsing-title requirement. MD3 transitions and Fluent theme brushes are approved native variants (#294).
- **R7. One selected-row rule for players and bands (#307, #318).** Solo and band song boards and the full instrument rankings board pin the selected profile's row above the pager on every page, the page that already shows the row included, with the same row component (web `FullRankingsPage` `hasPlayerFooter = !!playerRanking`; Android decides it with `RankingSpotlight.pinnedPlacement`, the overview cards with `RankingSpotlight.placement`). Only the Leaderboards overview cards hide a footer for a player already in their top rows (web `RankingCard`). A selected row shown apart from its page (Song Detail's appended row, a footer while the row is on another page) jumps to the page containing its rank and reveals the highlighted row once its own entrance has finished, rushing the rest of the page's stagger ([load-transition](load-transition.md) R5, #323; instant under Reduce Motion); once the row is on screen it opens the profile (Statistics for a player, the Band page for a band). Other rows open their player or band. Labels name the destination ("Jump to your band's position" / "Open band"). The selected player's own row inside a board or preview speaks its rank as "Your rank, 3rd" rather than "#3", like band rows' "Your band, …", so the purple highlight is never the only cue (Apple `SongLeaderboardEntryRow.spokenRank`, #457; HIG Color, *should*: "Avoid relying solely on color to differentiate objects… also convey it another way, like text labels"). The pinned row is one whole-row control like the rows above it: on the Mac it draws the keyboard focus ring in its card's shape, opens with Return as well as Space and tints on hover, never a plain button whose system ring the card hides (Apple `pinnedFooterControl`, #461; HIG Keyboards: "Support Full Keyboard Access"). **Owner-approved variant, Compete previews (#370):** the selected player's separate "your rank" row under a Compete leaderboard preview (shown because they rank outside its top rows) is a Song Detail-style preview row: it opens that instrument's full rankings board, for the card's metric, on the page containing the rank and reveals the highlighted row ("Jump to your position"), instead of opening the profile as the web `RankingCard` footer does. A selected row inside the preview's top rows and every other player's row still open the profile; combo previews take the variant too (#602), opening the combo's full board. Windows draws Compete's previews with the Leaderboards overview instrument cards, so those cards take the variant there; Apple's Compete cards take it on iPhone, iPad, Mac and the Duo carousel, while Apple's Leaderboards overview cards and Rival Detail previews keep the base rule; Android's Leaderboards overview cards, band ranking cards and other ranking previews keep the base rule. HIG Lists and tables: "Provide appropriate selection feedback."
- **R8. Stack score footers once per section, never truncate the score (#264).** A row's team score footer (web `SongBandScoreFooter`: score, then FC, accuracy and stars) keeps the score whole at every width and text size. When any realized row in a section (one Song Detail band preview card, one song band board) measures that its badges don't fit beside its score, every row in that section puts the badges on a line under the score; the plan is a measured section-wide decision (R1), made at measure time so virtualized rows report the stacked height at once, and rows grow (R2). Rows report only their own need, so the plan converges. Badges may clip at their own line's edge only at extreme sizes; the score never does. A new footer reuses the platform's canonical footer below instead of its own grid, wrap or trimming. WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize text); HIG Layout: "rows/containers may grow to avoid clipping/overlap and allow multiple lines."
- **R9. Apple preview rows group into one card (#381).** In Apple's dashboard previews (Song Detail, Compete, Leaderboards overview) the rows, skeletons, spotlight and message rows share one flush `FestivalGlassSection` card ([surface-materials](surface-materials.md) R8). The row reads `festivalGroupedRow` through `RankingRowSurface.treatment(isSelected:card:grouped:)`: a selected row fills its band in `playerFill`, others draw no card. Heights (R2), columns (R1) and selection (R7) are unchanged. Full boards (song boards, Full Rankings, Band Rankings) are not previews: they group each page's rows into one card under R10 (#543).
- **R10. Native boards group a page's rows into one card (owner #543).** On the native apps, a song board (Solo and band), Full Rankings and Band Rankings draw the page's rows in one card with hairline separators, like Song Detail's previews and Rivals; Song Detail's band previews do the same on Android, whose instrument previews were already one card. Windows follows in #643: Song Detail's instrument and band previews and each Leaderboards overview board (instrument, combo and Duos/Trios/Quads) are one `FSTCardStyle` card (padding 8,8,8,12) holding the loading placeholders (`LeaderboardsSkeleton.InCard`), the inline failure, the rows as in-card `GroupedRows` slices (`SongBandPreviewRowView` is an `IGroupedRow`), the overview's spotlight row (`LeaderboardsSpotlight.InCard`: the player's purple slice or a hairline-separated message row) and View All last; an empty board is the bare compact empty state with no card (#377). The First Run Song Info and Leaderboards/Compete demos draw the same card (`FirstRunDemo.RowsCard`). The selected row keeps its fill inside the card, while the pinned selected row and the pager stay a separate floating bar (R5, R7). The pinned row floats as its own selected-row fill alone, with no card, band or shadow around it (owner #579: "Selected profile doesn't need the card surrounding the purple"); Android draws it through `RankingsBoardScaffold.kt` `PinnedSelectedRow` (the 8 dp `PINNED_ROW_INSET` that aligns it with the list rows, no surface), keeping `AnchoredRowCard` only for the pinned message states (rank unavailable, unranked), as Apple's `bottomChrome` floats the row over the page (#93). Row height (R2), columns (R1), skeletons and the fade above the pager are unchanged. The web keeps its card per row. See the owner-approved variant below.
- **R11. Rank-history rows share fixed columns (#594).** A rank-history list (Leaderboards Rank History, the Profile rank chart's snapshots, Band Rank History) is one section (R1): its rank and value columns each take one width measured once over **every** chart day (not only the listed rows), in the bold weight the newest row draws, with tabular figures and right alignment, so the bold newest row, the other rows and the tapped bar's detail line up digit by digit (web `RankHistoryChart` / `BandRankHistoryChart`). The date takes the rest of the row. When large text leaves no room for the widest date beside the columns, every row moves its date above the columns and grows (R2); when even the two columns don't fit on one line, the rank and value each take their own line **at the same measured width**, right-aligned (never stretched to the row), so they still line up. The columns never shrink or clip (only a single column wider than the whole row is capped at the row and wraps), and the date never truncates. Every rank-history row and detail is at least the R2 48-unit baseline, owned by the shared row component rather than each consumer. Owner (#594): "Bolded rank is not vertically in line with other ranks (rank history)". WCAG 1.4.4 (Resize text).
- **R12. Don't let the pinned row cover the board at accessibility text sizes (#386).** At accessibility text sizes a selected row pinned under R7 that would take more than a third of the board's height follows the page's rows instead, at the end of the scrolling content above the pager, keeping its action and label. Default text sizes and short rows keep R7's pinning; an unmeasured row stays pinned. Apple decides it with `SelectedRowPinning.pins(isAccessibilitySize:rowHeight:boardHeight:)` on Solo and band song boards and Full Rankings. HIG Typography, Supporting Dynamic Type (iOS): "Adapt layouts for all Dynamic Type sizes" and "Maintain consistent information hierarchy at all sizes". Agent decision below. Journeys that look for the pinned row at AX5 accept it in the content when `pins` says it follows the rows (`SongLeaderboardEndJourneyTests`, `FullRankingsFooterJourneyTests` (#620) and the Duo pager `DuoBoardPagerAccessibilityJourneyTests` (#626)).
- **R13. A linked row is one accessibility element: its link (#675).** When a row opens something (a player's profile), the link's button is the row's only element and reads the row's columns as its label; don't also combine the row's columns inside it. Apple `RankingRowLayout(combinesAccessibility: false)` under `AccountRankingRow`'s profile link (the link carries the hidden-songs value, #38); anonymous rows and the pinned footer row, which have no link of their own, still combine. On iPad the link also carries the Open in New Window context menu (`openInNewWindowMenu`), and with it UIKit surfaced the nested combined element as a second full-row element inside the button; XCUITest's hit test then landed on that copy and reported the row unhittable while real taps opened the player (apple-ci iPad, release 2610.19). HIG VoiceOver: "Specify how elements are grouped, ordered or linked". Guarded on device by `GroupedCardAccessibilityJourneyTests` (`expectOneElementPerRow`, default size and AX5).

### Agent decision: Android large-text name budget (#188)

Agent decision (#188, 2026-10-06, owner may override with `/choose`): at large text (font scale 1.3 and up), stacked ranking rows on every surface (Leaderboards cards, Full Rankings, Band Rankings, Compete) reserve a fixed number of name lines with `minLines` and no line cap: two for players (`STACKED_RANKING_NAME_LINES`; Epic display names are at most 16 characters, which wrap to at most two lines at 2.0) and one per member for bands (`stackedBandNameLines`: Duos 2, Trios 3, Quads 4). `RankingsSkeletonRows` reserves the same budget, so the Leaderboards cards no longer grow when a wrapped name arrives (FST_Resizable desktop 2.0: a wrapped name made its row 180 vs 138 dp). Narrow stacked rows at normal text (#115, #116) have no skeleton and keep names at their own length.

| Option | Result | Guidance and precedent |
|---|---|---|
| A. One-line name budget (before) | Short names stay compact; a wrapped name grows its row and the card jumps after loading. | Breaks R2's no-jump rule. |
| **B. Fixed multi-line budget as a minimum (chosen)** | Rows in a section share one height and match the skeleton; one-line names leave a blank line at 2.0; rosters longer than the budget still grow. | R2 (rows don't jump), R3 (accessibility text wraps); MD3 Lists come in fixed "One-line, Two-line, Three-line" variants (consider); WCAG 1.4.4. |
| C. Cap names at the budget with an ellipsis | Fully deterministic heights. | R3 and WCAG 1.4.4: large text must wrap, not truncate. |

B wins: it is the only option that keeps both R2 and R3. Web rows never stack, so there is no web precedent; the trade-off is extra height per row at large text. Residual, accepted: a single member name wider than the whole name column (on FST_Phone at 2.0, about 15 wide characters) still wraps onto a line beyond the budget (one live Trios row of five, 4 lines in a 3-line budget). Reserving that worst case would double the blank space in every row, and one roster per line cannot help, because wrapping the joined roster never takes more lines than breaking it per member.

### Agent decision: shared-row names wrap at 150%+ text (#455)

Agent decision (Windows, #455, 2026-10-11, owner may override with `/choose`): every `LeaderboardEntryRow` name (player and band rows on every board, preview, spotlight and pinned row) sets `MarqueeText.WrapsAtLargeText`. At 150% Windows text and above (`Festival.Core.Domain.LargeText.Scale`, the [song-header](song-header.md) R3 threshold) a long name wraps onto more lines and the row grows (R2), still and whole, whatever the motion settings. Below 150% it marquees, and Reduce Motion or Animation effects off still ellipsize it. Before, Windows was the only platform whose names stayed on one line at large text. At 150–225% a too-wide name scrolled on one line, and with motion off it was cut to "…". The #455 accessibility tests exposed this.

| Option | Result | Guidance and precedent |
|---|---|---|
| A. Keep one line at every size (before) | Large text with motion off cuts the name; with motion on the reader chases a moving name. | Breaks R3 ("accessibility text wraps"). |
| **B. Wrap at the shared large-text threshold (chosen)** | Full name readable and still; the row grows like Apple's and Android's stacked rows. | R3; song-header R3 (the same `WrapsAtLargeText` switch and threshold); WCAG 1.4.4 Resize text; Microsoft Learn *Text scaling* (must): "As text is resized, controls and containers must also resize and reflow to accommodate the text and its new layout"; winui-design layout review, Reflow: "Sequence can wrap or change columns". |
| C. Wrap only with motion off | The moving one-line name remains at large text, and two layouts depend on a motion setting. | No precedent. |

**Large-text name budget (#455 design review, 2026-10-11, agent decision, owner may override with `/choose`).** While names wrap, every row in a section reserves the same number of name lines, and so does the section's skeleton, so a long name that arrives neither makes its row taller than its neighbours nor the card jump (R2). The budget is Android's #188 one: two lines for players (`LeaderboardRowMetrics.PlayerNameLines`; Epic names are at most 16 characters), one per member for bands with at least two (`BandNameLines`: Duos 2, Trios 3, Quads 4), and one for Score History date labels (`LabelNameLines`). Each row type states its budget (`ILeaderboardEntryRow.LargeTextNameLines`); `LeaderboardColumns.Measure` takes the section's largest and stores it in `LeaderboardSection.NameLines`, which `SkeletonSection`/`SkeletonRows` also take, so loaded, spotlight, pinned and skeleton rows share it. `LeaderboardEntryRow` sets it as the name's `MarqueeText.MinLines`, which reserves that many lines only while the text wraps (150%+), so 100–125% rows keep 48 epx. It is a minimum, never a cap: a name wider than the budget still wraps further (R3), as on Android. A name shorter than its budget is centred in the reserved lines, level with the row's centred rank. Trade-off: at 150%+ a short player name leaves one blank line and a Quad row reserves four, so a compact window at 225% shows fewer rows and cards (the large-text journeys scroll their target into view instead of expecting it on the first screen). Options weighed: A) no budget (each name chooses its height; rejected by the design review for breaking R2); B) a fixed budget per row kind (chosen; same values as Android, deterministic for skeletons that have no names); C) a width-aware budget measured from the loaded names (rejected: the skeleton draws before any name exists, so it can't match). Fluent *Text scaling* (must): "controls and containers must also resize and reflow"; WCAG 1.4.4. UIA reports a `MarqueeText`'s text bounds rather than its reserved height, so the journeys check the budget at the row: equal row heights and pitch.

Narrator reads the row's one name ("Rank #2, <name>. …"), and the name `MarqueeText` stays raw-view with the test ID `<row id>.name` (`LeaderboardRowIds.Name`). Tests: `LeaderboardNameMarkupTests`, `LeaderboardUnifyTests.NameIds_PairWithTheRowIds` and the budget's `LeaderboardSkeletonTests` (`NameBudgetIsTwoLinesForPlayersOnePerBandMemberAndOneForLabels`, `PlayerRowsPinnedRowAndSkeletonShareTheNameBudget`, `BandRowsReserveOneNameLinePerMember`, `SectionReservesTheMostLinesAnyRowAsksFor`) (unit), and `tools/windows/journeys/a11y-leaderboard-names.json`. `ui_ci.py` runs that journey five times: `leaderboard-names`, `leaderboard-names-text-150`, `leaderboard-names-text-200`, `leaderboard-names-text-225` and `leaderboard-names-no-animations`. They cover Full Rankings and the Leaderboards overview: moving or still per motion setting, wrapped at 150/200/225% (with Reduce Motion too), the read name, 40+ epx targets, rows apart, reading order and Up/Down focus. The budget pages (`lb-names-budget-*`, fixture `--max-name` gives rank 2 a 16-character name) assert at 150, 200 and 225% that the short, maximum-length and long-name rows are at least two body lines (50 epx) tall at one shared pitch, and that a loading overview card's skeleton rows keep that pitch and height.

### Agent decision: score rows stack instead of dropping the season (#170)

### Agent decision: Android rank-history columns and alignment audit (#594)

Agent decision (2026-10-10, owner may override with `/choose`): Android's three rank-history lists draw through one `HistoryColumnsRow` on a `HistoryColumnPlan` (R11).
