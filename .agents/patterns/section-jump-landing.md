# Section-jump landing

> **What:** where a Quick Links or Songs index jump places a section and how that section becomes current. **Read when:** adding a section jump, changing a scroll anchor, or changing the active-section line.

Status: **current**, 2026-10-11. Provenance: #9, #286, #298, #336, #388, #393, #415, #451, #582, #587.

## Intent

A jump names and exposes its destination immediately. A normal section lands below obscuring chrome; a pinned Songs title lands exactly where it pins, with its first row clear of the fade.

## Web source (behavior reference)

| Web | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `FortniteFestivalWeb/src/hooks/ui/usePageQuickLinks.ts` (`DEFAULT_QUICK_LINK_SCROLL_OFFSET = 32`, `DEFAULT_SONGS_SCROLL_OFFSET = 16`) | Computes the compact jump target, natural active item, and jump ownership. |
| `FortniteFestivalWeb/src/pages/songs/SongsPage.tsx` (`usePageQuickLinks`) | Supplies the Songs virtualizer's section targets and active item. |

The web has no drag section index. Native indexes reuse its section order and active-section contract.

## Rules

1. **R1. Share the landing policy.** Quick Links, a Songs index, and any programmatic section jump use the platform quick-links/section-header helper, never page-local offset arithmetic.
2. **R2. Land ordinary sections 32 units below chrome.** The target heading and active line use the same 32 pt/dp/epx offset; clamp near either content end and retain the visible target as current. HIG Layout: "Respect [the safe area] so system UI and hardware do not cover controls/content." The line is the section's *title*, not the padded top of the row or card that holds it: a heading's own top padding must not become dead space under chrome. On Android a page passes `headingInset` (the title's offset inside its lazy item or scroll section, e.g. `SECTION_HEADER_TOP_PADDING` 16 dp, Song Detail's instrument `CARD_HEADER_TOP_PADDING` 12 dp, Compete's 8 dp group header) to `rememberQuickLinks`/`rememberScrollQuickLinks`; `QuickLinksController` lands the item at 32 − inset (`QuickLinks.itemLandingPx`) and tracks the active line from the heading (`QuickLinks.headingFrame`). Pinned headers ignore it (R3). Page-local scroll arithmetic is still forbidden (R1) (#587: Song Detail's Bass jump left its header's 12 dp padding above the title; `QuickLinksLandingUiTest`, `SongsAccessibilityJourneyTest#songDetailQuickLinksJump*`).
3. **R3. Land pinned Songs titles at their pin line.** Apple `landingOffset` is zero; the title coincides with its pinned copy and the first row is unfaded (#298). Do not restore the superseded 30 pt workaround. The section index lands the same way: on Android an index jump scrolls to the section's sticky title item, not its first row, so the row sits clear below the pinned title (`WideColumnLines.itemOfSection`, #582).
4. **R4. Re-settle lazy targets.** Correct an estimated or newly realized target without changing sort, filters, selected profile, or triggering a read. Re-tapping a current index item still jumps.
5. **R5. Make target ownership explicit.** Select the target when the jump begins; hand back to natural tracking only after it leaves the landing/reachable band. Near-end targets remain current while visible. The newest jump owns correction and settling: an earlier jump's pending correction stops without scrolling or settling once another jump begins, so a held Next Section shortcut or a quick rotor flick ends on its last target (#393: the stale pass re-landed its old target and Quick Links named a section off screen). On Windows a Quick Links menu pick jumps only after the menu has closed and restored keyboard focus to `QuickLinksMenuButton`: where that button scrolls with the page, a restore that lands after the jump's scroll brings it back into view and hands the target back to the top section (#548: `qlj-profile-menu` at 225% text named Global Statistics after a Drums pick, whose section has no focusable control to take focus).
6. **R6. Keep the target perceivable.** The active link/index value names the landed section, and the heading remains an accessibility heading. Do not use fading to create clearance. A drag section index is one adjustable control assistive technologies present as one: on macOS SwiftUI's `accessibilityAdjustableAction` alone leaves a role-less `AXUnknown` element VoiceOver cannot offer to adjust, so the Mac describes `SongSectionIndexScrubber` as a slider over the sections (`AXSlider`, value = the label) and iOS keeps the adjustable trait (#451). HIG VoiceOver: "label any custom elements". Apple evidence: the hosted `SongsSectionJumpAccessibilityTests` (`apple-ci` on every change) steps the Songs index far forward and back and picks Year Quick Links, with default and with Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast settings, and checks the index/Quick Links value, one section-bar heading read before the rows, the landed heading on the bar's line and its first row a named ≥ 44 pt button right below the bar; `SongsChromeJourneyTests/testSectionJumpIsAccessibleAtLargestText` and `…DuoFolded` (CI journeys, large fixture) do the same at AX5 with the system audit (#451). Windows evidence: `tools/windows/journeys/a11y-section-index.json` `index-backward-after-scroll` and `-keyboard` (pinned title names B, reads "B, text", stays a Level 2 heading after a backward pick), run by the `windows-ui` CI workflow (`tools/windows/ui_ci.py` `RUNS`) at compact and medium, 100% and 225% text (#415). A Windows jump also moves keyboard focus into the landed section, and it does that only once the section's controls exist: a virtualizing list far below the viewport realizes them only after the scroll, so `QuickLinksBinder` retries after layout passes (#431, `qla-settings-menu` at 225% text).
7. **R7. Keep platform-native jump controls.** **Approved variants:** Apple uses `ListScrollNudger` for `List`; Android uses Compose `stickyHeader`; Windows uses `SemanticZoom` and `SongSectionHeader.JumpPinDelta` (#288). These vary presentation, not R1-R6. An index opens in its final layout: chrome around the list region must not resize that region while the index opens or closes. On Windows the pinned section bar hides as soon as the zoom starts (`ViewChangeStarted`, not `ViewChangeCompleted`) and hides by turning transparent and UIA Raw while keeping its height, so the vertically centred letters never re-centre (#648: the bar collapsed after the zoom-out, the index grew 35 epx and its letters jumped 17 epx at about 235 ms). Windows evidence: with `--perf-log` the Songs page traces each opening's frames (`jump-frame`), and `tools/windows/jump_trace.py` fails an opening whose size, cells, cell positions or scroll offset change after its first rendered frame; `a11y-section-index.json` pages set `"jump_trace": N` so `a11y_matrix.py` judges every opening. Fluent (`winui-design` layout review): a "stable size for the structural pane, flexible for content".
8. **R8. Fit the index to its region.** A drag section index is never taller than the height it is offered and never makes the page taller than its window; where every label does not fit, it keeps the first and last labels, evenly spaced ones between them and a bullet for each skipped run (the system table index's condensed form). A label lands on its own section, a drag still passes every section and the adjustable action still steps one at a time (#336: the rigid 27-label Apple strip pushed Duo outer-landscape Songs past the window, under the title and with the Filter field offscreen). HIG Designing for iPhone Duo: "The outer display is wider and shorter than other iPhone displays"; "avoid fixed widths or display-specific dependencies". Its labels follow the text size, with the same fit: on Apple they use the `caption2` text style capped at AX2 (218%), the strip widens with them but is never taller than at the default size, and at accessibility sizes it starts below the expanded search field (#388: the fixed 10 pt letters ignored Dynamic Type; uncapped condensed letters filled the column and overlapped the search field). HIG Accessibility: "Ideally, offer text enlargement of at least 200%".
9. **R9. Never rest part-way through a collapsing large title.** A programmatic scroll (a landing correction, a `.top` jump while the inset changes, a Quick Links settle) must not leave the list resting inside the large title's collapse band; UIKit snaps the title open or closed only when a *drag* ends. Once the list has been still for 400 ms (no finger down, no deceleration), Apple finishes the collapse at the nearer end, like the drag snap: `LargeTitleRest` decides, `LargeTitleRestWatch` times it and `ListScrollNudger.settleLargeTitle(at:)` animates the offset. It works on every supported system: iOS 18+ reads `onScrollGeometryChange` and the idle scroll phase; iOS 17 (and `FST_DEBUG_LEGACY_SCROLL_GEOMETRY=1`) reads the List's platform scroll view through the scroll-edge legacy path (`EnclosedScrollViewLocator` + `PlatformScrollObserver`) and waits out `isTracking`/`isDragging`/`isDecelerating` itself (#608 review: an iOS-18-only watch left iOS 17 unfixed) (#560: the title sat half-way up behind Open Navigation and the "#" title under the Filter field). The band is known only after both ends have been seen at the current width, so a list the bar does not track, or one with no collapsing title (macOS, Duo, the iPhone's own pose since #632's leading inline title, Android, Windows), never moves. The iPhone bundle's park journeys (`SongsChromeJourneyTests.testProgrammaticScrollNeverRestsPartWay…`) therefore run in the simulated folded window, where the same build keeps Songs' system large title, Open Navigation and Profile; there the Filter field is the bottom field, so they check "#" below the navigation bar's drawn bottom and the field below it (#678). HIG Motion: "make feedback realistic and consistent with gestures and expectations" (should).
