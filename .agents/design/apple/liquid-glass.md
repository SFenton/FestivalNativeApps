# Liquid Glass on Apple platforms (iOS/iPadOS/macOS 26+)

> **What:** Lane A's decision record for which surfaces are Liquid Glass, which use the shared material card, and the shared components. **Read when:** adding any container, toolbar item, sheet or floating control on an Apple platform.

Decision record for **which surfaces are Liquid Glass**, owned by Lane A (Shell), 2026-09-27. Every Apple lane follows this table; propose changes to the orchestrator rather than diverging per page.

## Principle

Apple's HIG for iOS 26 puts Liquid Glass on the **navigation/control layer that floats above content** (tab bars, toolbars, sheets, menus, floating controls) and keeps **content** legible and non-refractive. Festival is unusual: every page's content already floats over the shared, animated album-art background (`FestivalBackgroundHost`), and the web renders its containers as frosted glass (`FrostedCard`, `fx.navFrosted`). Content still follows HIG Materials ("**Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer.** Use standard materials for content-layer elements"): the web's frosted look is reproduced with standard materials, not Liquid Glass. *Supersedes* the 2026-09-27 rationale that content cards and song rows be Liquid Glass at the operator's request; that was reversed by the material card (operator, 2026-10-03/04, issue #291). The cross-platform rules live in the [surface-materials pattern](../../patterns/surface-materials.md). The resolution:

1. **System chrome is always system glass.** Never hand-build a tab bar, nav bar, search field, sheet or menu to get a glass look; use SwiftUI's and restyle nothing but tint/foreground.
2. **Content containers that the web draws as frosted cards use the shared material card** (`festivalCard` / `FestivalGlassSection`, [Material card](#material-card)), not per-card Liquid Glass (issue #291, 2026-10-04). HIG Materials: "**Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer.** Use standard materials for content-layer elements such as app backgrounds." They were tinted glass cards until then; each live `glassEffect` cost main-thread time while scrolling.
3. **Custom (non-system) floating controls use the material capsule** (`festivalCardCapsule`), not hand-built glass: section-index scrubber, rankings pager and switcher pills, Paths pickers, the unranked pill. HIG Materials: "Use Liquid Glass effects sparingly on custom controls; system components provide the appearance and behavior automatically." They keep ≥ 44 pt hit targets and their own press feedback. The hamburger drawer is navigation, not a control, and stays glass ("Use Liquid Glass for controls and navigation (such as tab bars and sidebars) floating above content").
4. **Never glass-on-glass, never a card in a card.** Anything inside a card (chips, pills, badges, meters, nested cards) uses flat fills. Glass inside a glass sheet is also forbidden.
5. **Status and data must stay ≥ 4.5:1.** Warnings, errors, scores and rank numbers never depend on what is refracted behind them.
6. **Accessibility overrides win.** System Reduce Transparency, the in-app *Reduce Transparency* and *Increase Contrast* toggles all replace glass and material with opaque `BrandTokens.cardBackground` + `borderSubtle`. `festivalCard`, `festivalCardCapsule`, `FestivalGlassSection`, `festivalGlass`, `festivalSheet` and the drawer already do this — do not bypass them with raw `.glassEffect` or a raw material.

## Shared components (use these, not raw modifiers)

- `festivalRootChrome(session:)` (`App/Shell/RootChrome.swift`) — apply on each **tab root only**. Adds the hamburger (leading, iPhone only) and profile avatar (trailing, top-right). The profile sheet and drawer are owned by `FestivalRootView`; call `@Environment(\.openProfile)` / `@Environment(\.openDrawer)` instead of presenting your own profile sheet. The avatar uses `@Environment(\.profileButtonAction)`: the selected player's Statistics, or the sheet when anonymous (issue #290).
- `FestivalGlassSection(_:subtitle:content:)` (`Design/GlassSection.swift`) — titled material card (the name predates issue #291) with white Title Case header outside the card, inset hairlines (iOS 18+), 44 pt minimum rows. `FestivalFootnote` for muted explanations inside.
- `festivalSheet(_:)` (`Design/SheetStyle.swift`) — dark sheet style with detents (`.large` default, `.compact` = medium + large) and a visible drag indicator. Apply to the sheet content root: `.sheet(isPresented: $x) { MySheet().festivalSheet(.compact) }`.
- `FestivalModal(_:closeIdentifier:path:onClose:content:)` (`Design/FestivalModal.swift`) — **every** modal's container (issue #23): `NavigationStack` (optionally with an `[AppRoute]` path), inline title and the system Close (`FestivalSheetCloseItem`: `Button(role: .close)` on iOS/macOS 26+, "Close" text before) top-right. Never hand-draw a ✕, a text Close/Done or a bottom Close in a modal; put extra toolbar items, `.searchable` and destinations on the content. Pair with `festivalSheet` (or the modal's own detents). Its content never draws under the header and fades in below it once scrolled, and the header has no glass band (`ModalTopEdgeFadeModifier`, issue #94); a sheet that builds its own `NavigationStack` (the feedback form) or pushes pages inside a modal (Find Rival) applies the same modifier. Measure the header as the larger of the content's top safe-area inset (read on a safe-area-respecting background: a mask's geometry, and any view that ignores the safe area, reports a zero inset) and its offset below the container (read in a named coordinate space; global frames include the sheet transform). Never add them: Paths content sits below the bar yet still reports the full inset (iOS 26.5). HIG Toolbars: "Close dismisses a modal; prefer their standard symbols without text labels." The hamburger drawer is a side navigation panel, not a modal, and keeps its own close. **Exception:** the feedback form ([feedback-form](../../controls/feedback-form/ios.md)) is a scoped task that can lose typed input, so it uses Cancel (leading) and Submit (trailing) with a discard confirmation instead of Close (HIG Sheets: "Single-view sheets: Cancel on the top toolbar's leading edge; Done, when present, trailing.").
- `festivalCard(cornerRadius:)` (default 16), `festivalCardCapsule()`, `festivalRowCard(cornerRadius:)` (default 12, Song and leaderboard rows) (`Design/RowCardSurface.swift`) — the shared [material card](#material-card) for every content card and custom control.
- `festivalGlass(_:cornerRadius:interactive:)`, `festivalGlassCapsule`, `FestivalGlassGroup` (`Design/GlassSurface.swift`) — the only place that calls `.glassEffect`. Shipping UI uses it only for the drawer (`.overlay`); `.card`/`.control` remain for the Debug A/B switch and capture canaries.

## Section headers

White (`textPrimary`), `.headline`, **Title Case**, outside the card, with an optional muted `subheadline` description — i.e. `FestivalSectionHeader`. Never the system's grey uppercase list headers; set `.textCase(nil)` if a system container would upper-case them.

## Performance and verification

- Glass is not free: no per-row `glassEffect` in long lists. Per-row glass also skipped the rows' staggered load-in fade, so the non-glass selected-player row faded in last (issue #295); leaderboard rows now share the material row card too. Since issue #291 no content card or custom control uses `glassEffect` ([material card](#material-card)); long lists use one group card (`FestivalGlassSection`) or flat rows. Measure with `tools/apple_perf.py --stress` (Songs) or `--stress --env FST_DEBUG_PAGE_SCROLL_STRESS=1` (Leaderboards, Profile, Settings) before adding a per-row surface.
- Verify each surface with `python3 tools/ios_sim.py shot` over **busy artwork** (Songs) and over the plain background (Settings); a surface that is only legible over the plain background fails.
- Reduce Transparency: with the in-app toggle on, every card, the drawer and sheets must render opaque.

## Material card

Operator decision 2026-10-03: a cheaper Songs row card with the same look (Lane CARD). Issue #291 (2026-10-04) extended it to every content card (`festivalCard`, `FestivalGlassSection`) and custom control (`festivalCardCapsule`) on every Apple page. HIG `materials.md`: "**Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer.** Use standard materials for content-layer elements such as app backgrounds"; a Song row is content, so the row moved from tinted Liquid Glass to a standard material.

| Layer | iOS / iPadOS 26+ | macOS 26+ |
|---|---|---|
| Material | `.ultraThinMaterial` (app scheme: dark) | same |
| Tint above it | sRGB (15, 17, 23) at 68% (88% with system Increase Contrast) | neutral (138, 135, 138) at 17% |
| Rim | 1 pt `strokeBorder`, white 14% → 3% top to bottom | white 6% → 0% (glass shows almost no edge) |
| Reduce Transparency (system or in-app), in-app Increase Contrast | opaque `cardBackground` + `borderSubtle` (pixel-identical to the glass card's fallback) | same |
| iOS 17–25 / macOS 14–25 | `surfaceFrosted` + `.ultraThinMaterial` + `glassBorder` (unchanged) | same |

- **Fitted, not guessed.** The Debug A/B switch `FST_DEBUG_ROW_CARD_AB=<file>` draws the old glass card (capsules: `.control` glass) on every material surface while the file says `glass`; flip it between two window captures with `FST_DEBUG_STILL_BACKGROUND=1` for pairs over an identical backdrop. iOS glass stays dark over the dimmed artwork (`cardBackground` as the tint read ~8 levels too blue); macOS glass reads lighter than its backdrop, so the Mac uses a light veil instead.
- **Plain modifiers only.** Material, tint and rim are three `background(_:in:)`/`overlay` modifiers; the same layers inside a nested `.background { … }` builder cost about as much as the glass. Tint and rim are free (iPad sim: material only 8.63 ms/row, + tint 8.70, + rim 8.64).
- **Cost:** see [architecture.md § Performance](../../platforms/apple/architecture.md#performance) (iPad −15%, iPhone −11%, Mac −8% main-thread time per row built; Mac stress stalls about halved; issue #291 card pages on iPad: Leaderboards −72%, Statistics −26%, Settings −32% main-thread time per stress pass).
- **Capture tests (issue #122):** GitHub's macOS runner reports system Reduce Transparency on, so a hosted card there correctly draws the opaque fallback, and AppKit draws a bare material opaque gray. `RowCardSurfaceTests` pins SwiftUI's Reduce Transparency off (`_accessibilityReduceTransparency`) for the material checks and asserts relatively on every host: the card centre matches its own tint-over-material layers drawn directly (≤ 1%) and is nearer them than the opaque fallback; the white page showing through is asserted only where a bare `ultraThinMaterial` over white captures bright (developer Macs).
- **Known difference (macOS, bright art):** Liquid Glass passes more of the artwork's brightness and detail through than the thinnest material. Over the brightest, most colourful covers the Mac card reads up to ~15 levels darker and more frosted; over dark covers ~6 levels lighter (mean error over 8 backdrops < 1 level). iOS/iPadOS pairs differ by 1–3 levels. Evidence: `~/FestivalShowcase/native-perf/card/`.
- System Increase Contrast (no in-app toggle): Liquid Glass darkens itself by 6–8 levels, so the iOS/iPadOS card uses an 88% tint there (pairs within 2–3 levels); the row still adds its own white 2 pt outline. The Mac keeps its veil (the system setting is not toggled on this host).
- First-run demo replicas (`FirstRunNativeSongsDemos` and the other demos) use the same material card as the surfaces they show (issue #291).
- 2026-10-04 (operator): the material Songs row card (`Design/RowCardSurface.swift`) is accepted on all Apple platforms, including the Mac's darker/more frosted look over bright covers; don't revert to per-row Liquid Glass.
