# Songs Section Index — iPhone notes

> **What:** the right-edge "drag to jump" scrubber over the Songs List. **Read when:** changing Songs sorting/sectioning or the scrubber itself. New in Wave 1 (Lane S); no `spec.md` exists yet because the web app has no equivalent control.

- `FestivalCore/SongSectionIndex.swift` groups an already-sorted, already-filtered `[Song]` into `SongSection`s by first letter (Title/Artist) or exact year (Year); Duration/Item Shop sorts return no sections. It chunks on **consecutive** key changes only (never merges by unique key) and never re-sorts, so it must run after `SongCatalogSort.sorted`. `SongSection.id` is a position index, not the label, because the same label can legitimately recur in two separate chunks.
- **Label rule (native Contacts convention, not a skip-ahead scan):** `firstLetter` diacritic-folds the title/artist's first character (`Ö` → `O`) and uses it as the label only when it is `A`–`Z`; any other leading character — punctuation, a digit, a symbol — shares one `"#"` bucket. It does **not** skip past a non-letter prefix to find "a real letter" deeper in the string. An earlier version did that and broke on the real 728-song catalogue: a raw-string title sort puts every non-letter-leading title (`"(Don't Fear)…"`, `"2055"`, `"24K Magic"`, `"4 Raws"`, `"500lbs"`, …) before any letter-led title, and skip-ahead gave each one an unrelated label (`"D"`, `"#"`, `"K"`, `"R"`, …) — scrambling the whole top of the scrubber (`D # K L R L F B T A…`) instead of one clean leading `"#"`. The current rule keeps every such title in that one leading `"#"` section, since that is where they already sort.
- Chunking by consecutive key (not by unique key) is a second, independent safeguard: it keeps two non-adjacent runs that happen to share a label (e.g. two separated `"#"` stretches) from merging into one out-of-position section. It is no longer load-bearing for the specific numeral-title scenario above once the label rule itself stopped manufacturing spurious letters, but a corrupt/foreign sort order could still produce non-adjacent same-label runs, so it stays.
- `Features/Songs/SongSectionIndexScrubber.swift` is a custom SwiftUI control (not the UIKit `UITableView` section-index API, which SwiftUI's `List` doesn't expose): a `festivalCardCapsule()` material strip of section labels (issue #291; HIG Materials: Liquid Glass "sparingly on custom controls"), driven by a `DragGesture(minimumDistance: 0)` that maps touch Y to a section and calls back with its `id`; `UISelectionFeedbackGenerator` fires on each section change. VoiceOver gets `.accessibilityAdjustableAction` (increment/decrement steps one section) instead of relying on the drag gesture.
- **Sized to its own content, not the List's height (fixed 2026-09-28):** an earlier version wrapped the whole strip in `GeometryReader { geometry in … .frame(height: geometry.size.height) }`, stretching the capsule to fill whatever height the enclosing `ZStack`/List proposed. On iPhone (and worse, iPhone Duo folded) that height isn't stable: it grows as the navigation bar's large title collapses (the List gets more room), so the strip visibly crept upward and its labels spread out past the first (`#`)/last (`Z`) entries, overlapping rows below. The fix drops that outer `GeometryReader`/forced `.frame(height:)` entirely — the `VStack` of labels now sizes intrinsically (as tall as its own letters), and the caller's `ZStack(alignment: .trailing)` centers it, which stays stable regardless of List height changes. A `GeometryReader` still exists, but only in a `.background` used to read the strip's *own* rendered height for the drag-to-section math — it is never fed back into the strip's frame. Vertical-bar clearance comes from the List's safe area, which on iPhone Duo (folded, or inner landscape) already ends at the system vertical bar; the strip's trailing margin is only `max(2, deviceLayout.cutoutInsets.trailing)` (`trailingMargin`), adding room for a hardware cutout beyond the safe area. Do not use `overlayInsets.trailing` here: it counts the bar twice and leaves a bar-wide gap (duo.md B4).
- **Never taller than offered; condenses to fit (issue #336, 2026-10-07).** The intrinsic capsule (27 Title labels: 362 pt, 378 pt with its 8 pt outer padding) is wrapped in `.frame(minHeight: 0, maxHeight: .infinity)`, so it takes exactly the height the caller's region offers and can never grow the `ZStack` or the page. On the iPhone Duo outer display in landscape (about 300 pt offered) the rigid strip had made the Songs page taller than the window: rows slid under the title, the bottom Filter field left the screen, and the overflow shift changed as the large title collapsed. `onGeometryChange` reads the offered height; `capacity(availableHeight:labelHeight:)` counts the rows that fit (the first label's measured line height, 12 pt at 10 pt rounded), and `entries(labels:capacity:)` keeps every label when they fit; otherwise it keeps the first and last labels and evenly spaced ones between them, with a `•` row standing for each skipped run (`Entry.sections`), which is the system table index's condensed form. A label row selects its own section (issue #9); a bullet row spreads its skipped sections across its height (`section(at:height:inset:entries:)`), so a drag still passes every section, and the adjustable action still steps one section at a time. The capsule itself stays intrinsic and centred (the 2026-09-28 rule above), so the strip shows the full list wherever it fits: iPhone, iPad, the inner display and outer portrait. HIG Designing for iPhone Duo: "The outer display is wider and shorter than other iPhone displays"; "avoid fixed widths or display-specific dependencies". Tests: `SongSectionIndexScrubberFitTests` (capacity, condensed rows, every section reachable, labels land on themselves) and the hosted `sectionIndexScrubberNeverOutgrowsItsOfferedHeight` (macOS).
- `SongsScreen.populatedList(payload:visible:effectiveMode:)` wraps the `List` in a `ScrollViewReader` and shows the scrubber in a trailing `ZStack` overlay only when `SongSectionIndex.sections(...).count > 1`; `.animation(_:value:)` on that boolean fades it in/out when the sort mode changes to/from Duration, Item Shop or a single-letter result set. Selecting a section calls `scrollProxy.scrollTo(section.id, anchor: .top)`; each section's `List` `Section` carries `.id(section.id)`.
- **Jumps land where tapped and name the section (issue #9):**
  - `SongSectionIndexScrubber.sectionIndex(at:height:inset:count:)` maps a touch over the labels only, excluding the 6pt `labelInset` padding at each end, and clamps. Before, it divided the whole padded height, so a tap near either end selected a neighbour (a tap on W gave V).
  - Each new touch jumps, even to the letter already selected. Before, re-tapping a letter after scrolling by hand did nothing. Haptic feedback still fires only when the selection changes.
  - `SongsScreen.jumpToSection` marks the target in the section bar (`SongsScrollChrome.jump(to:in:)`), scrolls instantly, then scrolls once more after 50ms unless a newer jump started. A far jump first lands on estimated row heights, so the second scroll corrects it.
  - **Jumps land where the title pins (issue #298, 2026-10-04).** The #286 landing below (kept for history) left dead space above the title, which then slid up on the next scroll. `SongsScrollChrome.landingOffset` is now 0 and the row fade shrinks out of the first row's way instead (`fadeLimit`, see [pages/songs/ios.md](../../pages/songs/ios.md)); `settleLanding` still runs, now only to correct far jumps placed from estimated heights. Quick Links on Duration/Item Shop use the same line.
  - **Superseded: jumps land below the soft edge (issue #286, 2026-10-03).** A flush `.top` landing put the title under the floating section bar and the first rows under the bar's 28 pt fade (iOS 26 soft scroll-edge plus the row mask, now `SectionBarRowFade`), so the destination looked partly faded; scrolling it clear moved the title back below the bar's line and the bar named the previous letter. Now the title lands on `SongsScrollChrome.landingOffset(fade:)` below the List's top inset (30 pt: under the bar's own label, its text blanked while passed; 2 pt with Reduce/Less Transparency or Increase Contrast, where the fade is a hard cut), so the first row starts below the fade, and `headerPassed` uses the same line. A `List` centres any custom `UnitPoint` anchor, so the jump scrolls `.top`, then `settleLanding` reads the target title's measured top (`watchLanding`/`recordTitleTop`) and moves the List onto the line with `ListScrollNudger` (up to 10 rounds of 32 ms; stops when landed, when the List cannot move, or on a newer jump). Quick Links on Duration/Item Shop use the same line. HIG Layout: "Respect [the safe area] so system UI and hardware do not cover controls/content"; HIG Materials: "scroll-edge effects add blur and reduce opacity". Verified on iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone Duo, iPad (live service) and macOS (hosted `List`, with and without the glass fallback).
  - The last letters, whose few songs cannot reach the top, leave the section at the top named (Z shows "Y").
  - The bar's own title threshold is described in [pages/songs/ios.md](../../pages/songs/ios.md).
- Sections show only letters/years actually present in the current results (not a full dimmed A–Z like Contacts) — a deliberate scope decision for a fixed, small catalogue rather than a sparse contacts list.
- Extracted into its own `populatedList` method (rather than inline in `body`) specifically because the extra `ScrollViewReader`/`ZStack`/`Section` nesting risked Swift's type-checker timing out inside the already-large Songs `switch`/`if` tree.

Like the #5 test, it needs `tools/mock_service.py --large-catalogue` exported as `TEST_RUNNER_FST_SONGS_SCROLL_FIXTURE_URL` and skips on the two-song fixture. Visually confirmed on the real catalogue via `tools/ios_sim.py shot --tab songs` and the `drive` accessibility-tree dump (`fst.songs.section-index`, `fst.songs.section.<n>`, `fst.songs.row.<songId>`).
- Open finding (#452, iPhone 17 Pro, iOS 26.5, AX XXXL, 2026-10-08): with the large title expanded at the top of the list, the rail's first letters (`#`–`D`, rail top y≈240) sit beside the grown Filter Songs field. The field doesn't reach them visually but its hit area does: tapping `B` focused the search field instead of jumping. VoiceOver's adjustable action still reaches every section, and after any scroll the title collapses and every letter is tappable.
